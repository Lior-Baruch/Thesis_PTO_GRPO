Win/lose directions, re-checked on our data

We re-ran the deck’s quantities on the GRPO K=0 and K=5 rounds with one encoder, gte-base, and added three checks. We removed the replies that carry a leaked chat marker or degenerate text, corrected the cosines for sampling noise, and tested the K=0-vs-K=5 reading against text features that were fixed on 29 September, before the deck.

What we ran

Rounds
The 29,861 train rounds with at least two distinct rewards (K=0 15,726, K=5 14,135), holding 238,888 candidates. The embeddings come from the 29 September analysis.
Best and worst
When several candidates share the best (or worst) reward, their embeddings are averaged. The deck takes a single best and a single worst.
Sentence pool
Your recipe: sentences of 4–10 words seen at least 3 times, capped at 4,488. Only 4,178 sentences reach 3 occurrences on our rounds (out of 225,240 distinct), and 3.1% of them contain a chat marker.
Variants
All candidates; leaked removed (any candidate containing <|im_…); leaked and degenerate removed. The filtered variants re-pick best and worst among the candidates that remain.
Noise-corrected cosine
Patients are split in half 50 times. Corrected cosine = cos(half A of one direction, half B of the other) ÷ √(reliability₁ × reliability₂), where reliability is a direction’s own split-half cosine. A value near 1 means the same direction once sampling noise is removed.

Finding 1

Leaked chat markers do not lose

  • K=0 leaks a lot. 19.9% of K=0’s candidates carry a leaked marker, rising to 58.1% at iteration 7 and 50.1% at iteration 8. K=5: 1.7%.
  • The leak is not on the losing side. Among K=0’s best candidates 21.3% leak, among its worst 19.3%. The within-round correlation with reward is +0.05.
  • Removing leaked candidates barely moves the directions. Their cosine with the all-candidates direction averages 0.96 for K=0 (lowest 0.83) and is at least 0.99 for K=5.
  • Yet markers crowd the lose end of the sentence pool. 45–48% of each arm’s 10 lowest-projecting sentences contain one, against 3.1% of the pool. With leaked candidates removed from the direction it is still 44–47%. With degenerate candidates removed as well it drops to 12–19%.

Reading. The direction pushes away from degenerate text, which is several times more common among the worst candidates than among the best (K=0 6.2% vs 1.6%, K=5 5.2% vs 1.9%). In gte space, sentences that carry a marker sit near garbled text, so they surface at the lose end even though leaked replies themselves win and lose at the base rate. The marker-heavy lose lists describe the encoder’s geometry. The reward does not penalise leaked replies.

Finding 3

Most of the late divergence and drift is noise

  • The raw numbers match the deck. K=0 and K=5 agree early (raw cosine 0.83–0.87 at iterations 1–3), and the raw cosine falls late (−0.03 at iteration 9, 0.12 at iteration 10).
  • K=5 stops having a direction. Its reliability drops from 0.87 at iteration 4 to 0.17 and 0.07 at iterations 9–10, so there is little left to agree with. Its top sentences at iterations 9–10 are noise.
  • The divergence at iterations 4–8 is real but partial. The noise-corrected cosine is 0.48–0.79 there.
  • Each arm is stable once noise is removed. The raw cosine between consecutive iterations falls (K=0 from 0.92 at iteration 2 to 0.70 at iteration 7; K=5 from 0.92 at iteration 3 to 0.18 at iteration 10). Noise-corrected it is 0.91–0.98 for K=0 through iteration 8 and 0.77–1.03 for K=5. K=5’s values at iterations 9–10 rest on reliabilities of 0.17 and 0.07, so they say little.

Finding 4

K=0’s iteration 9 is a real reversal

  • It is reliable. K=0’s iteration-9 direction has split-half reliability 0.77 over 1,200 rounds, and its cosine with iteration 8 is −0.30 (noise-corrected −0.32). It is the drop in Figure 3, bottom left.
  • The grader failure cannot explain it. Only 32 candidates lost their reward that iteration, and they are excluded from every round.
  • Agreement wins that iteration. Its winning sentences are agreement replies: Absolutely, you’re absolutely right. I understand exactly where you’re coming from.
  • Iteration 10 follows neither. Its direction is unrelated to iteration 9’s (cosine −0.08).

Finding 5

K0 − K5: the praise side replicates, the reframing side only partly

  • The K=0 side is praise and support in most iterations, with or without the filters: I couldn’t be more proud of you. You have my full support and unconditional acceptance.
  • The K=5 side is mixed. It has reframes and open questions (What other ideas come to mind?, So, instead of focusing on what you need to do,; iterations 3 and 5). It also has tips (One tip is to keep a food journal., Remember, consistency is key.) and smoking-cessation topic sentences (iterations 4 and 9).

  • Text features fixed on 29 September (Figure 4), each correlated with a candidate’s projection on K0 − K5 within each arm’s own candidates, leaked and degenerate candidates removed:
    • praise phrase lines up with the K=0 side from iteration 5 on, mostly inside K=0’s own candidates: +0.19 to +0.53 at iterations 5, 6, 8 and 10, against +0.05 to +0.15 in K=5’s;
    • reflection opener stays near zero throughout (−0.11 to +0.10);
    • length is the strongest correlate. Longer replies sit on the K=5 side in most iterations; the two sign flips (iterations 7 and 10) come from K=0’s candidates (+0.49, +0.40).

Reading. “K=0’s reward pulls toward validation and praise” holds. “K=5’s reward pulls toward reframing” is too strong. The data support a narrower claim: K=5’s reward lacks K=0’s pull toward praise, and its side of the difference mixes questions, reframes, tips and topic content.

Caveats

  • One encoder. The deck’s claim that the result holds in every embedder is not tested here. gte is an external sentence encoder, like Qwen3 and mxbai.
  • The directions are weak per round. On 29 September, a held-out direction picked K=0’s winner in about 69% of rounds and ranked a round’s 8 candidates at Spearman ≈ 0.2. The mean direction summarises a tendency; it does not decide most rounds.
  • Top-sentence lists are read by eye. Only the keyword counts and the feature correlations are quantitative, and the keyword lists were written after the sentences had been seen.
