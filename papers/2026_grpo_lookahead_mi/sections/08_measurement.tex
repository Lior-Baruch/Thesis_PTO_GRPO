\section{Where the ruler stops working}
\label{sec:measurement}

The result in §\ref{sec:reward} rests on two graders agreeing about an ordering. This section
reports, at the level of individual conversations, where they stop agreeing --- because that place
turns out to be the winning checkpoint itself, and a paper that reported the gain without it would
be hiding its own sharpest caveat.

\subsection{Arm-level agreement is strong}

Across the parent experiment's full grid, the held-out judge preserves the sign of
$6{,}693$ of $8 \times \binom{44}{2} = 8 \times 946 = 7{,}568$ pairwise arm $\times$ instrument
contrasts, or $88.4\%$; restricted to contrasts the training oracle scores at
$|\Delta| \geq 0.25$ this rises to $97.2\%$, and at $|\Delta| \geq 0.50$ to $99.3\%$. The two
graders disagree, in other words, mainly about differences too small to claim. Every contrast this
paper reports is well inside the range where they agree.

\subsection{Per-conversation agreement collapses at the winning checkpoint}

That statistic is \emph{arm-level}, and it does not license a per-conversation claim. On the
rewarded rubric Q1, per-conversation agreement between the two graders along the $\Kf$ arm reads
$0.941$ at iteration 5, then $0.877$, $0.842$, $0.769$, and falls to $\mathbf{0.487}$ at iteration
9 and $\mathbf{0.544}$ at iteration 10 --- against a median of $0.855$ across all 44 model states
of the parent experiment. \textbf{These are the two lowest-agreeing of the 44 states on Q1}
(the qualifier matters: MITI and MICI reach lower absolute correlations at this same state, but
they do so in every arm --- see Table~\ref{tab:agreement}). The best checkpoint by score is the
checkpoint the two graders most disagree about, conversation by conversation.

The decline is also not unique to this arm. Both $K{=}5$ arms lose Q1 agreement over their last two
iterations (PTO's $K{=}5$ runs $0.852 \rightarrow 0.797 \rightarrow 0.667$) while neither $K{=}0$
arm does. With two arms per optimizer we cannot separate "a property of this checkpoint" from "a
property of long $K{=}5$ training", and we flag it as the more interesting of the two readings
rather than asserting either.

The collapse is selective, but not in the way one might assume, and comparing raw correlations
across instruments would get it backwards --- the eight rubrics differ in baseline agreement by
more than the effect, so each must be read against its own distribution. Doing that
(Table~\ref{tab:agreement}), the disagreement is \textbf{not} confined to the rewarded rubric. MITI
falls furthest from its own median ($-0.325$) and reaches its \emph{worst value across all 44 model
states} at this exact cell; Q1 is next ($-0.311$, its second-worst state); MICI and Q2 follow. The
four patient-perspective Likert instruments barely move ($-0.02$ to $-0.05$).

The pattern that holds is that the graders stop agreeing on the two \emph{rewarded} rubrics and on
the two \emph{behaviour-coding} rubrics, and keep agreeing on the global-impression ones. That is
consistent with two distinct causes stacking at the same checkpoint: saturation on what was
optimised (§\ref{sec:measurement}, below), and the known difficulty of cross-model behaviour
coding, where MITI is the least dependable instrument in the battery experiment-wide
(single-grader dependability $0.624$, against $0.91$--$0.97$ for the Likert rubrics). We report
both rather than attributing all of it to the reward.

\subsection{The mechanism is one-sided saturation, not homogenised output}

A correlation can collapse because the underlying conversations became uniform, or because one
rater ran out of range. Here it is the second, and the two are distinguishable --- because
\textbf{only one of the two graders' spreads moves}.

The training oracle's Q1 standard deviation falls monotonically across $\Kf$'s run,
$1.336$ at base to $0.701$ at iteration 10 (Spearman $\rho = -0.86$ between SD and iteration,
$p = .001$). That is a variance ratio of $0.701^2 / 1.336^2 = 0.275$, and it is not an artifact of
which iteration one anchors on: against iteration 1 it is $0.285$, against the mean of iterations
1--10 it is $0.544$. It has lost most of its discriminative range as its scores pile up near the
top of the scale.

On the \emph{identical conversations}, the held-out judge's Q1 spread does not move: $\rho = +0.44$,
$p = .18$, hovering between $0.76$ and $1.00$ with no trend. \todo{earlier drafts said this grader's
variance \emph{grew} $1.410\times$. That figure anchors on iteration 0, which happens to be the
series minimum; re-anchored to iteration 1 it is $1.062\times$ and against the mean of iterations
1--10 it is $1.034\times$. Flat is the honest description, and it is all the argument needs --- do
not reinstate the growth claim.}

Flat is sufficient. Had the conversations themselves become homogeneous, \emph{both} graders'
spreads would have shrunk. One shrank and the other did not, on the same 96 conversations, so what
changed is the ruler and not the thing being measured.

\subsection{What this does and does not undermine}

It does not undermine the headline. The held-out judge, which is the one still discriminating,
independently ranks $\Kf$ above $\Kz$ at $\dz\,1.030$ --- a \emph{larger} standardised effect than
the saturating grader reports. An arm-level ordering that both graders agree on, one of them from
an unsaturated range, is exactly the claim that survives.

It does undermine three things, and we mark them wherever they appear. First, any per-conversation
claim about $\Kf$'s late checkpoints on the four depressed instruments --- Q1, Q2, MITI and MICI.
This is why §\ref{sec:behaviour}'s behavioural argument is built on the deterministic lexical
marker and not on the MITI/MICI counts: the two rubrics carrying the coded-behaviour signal are
precisely the two whose cross-grader agreement is worst at this checkpoint. Second, the absolute
endpoint level $4.517$, which sits
in the compressed part of the training oracle's range and should be read as "near the top of this
grader's scale", not as a calibrated score. Third, the precise magnitude of the residual
MI-inconsistency in §\ref{sec:behaviour-honest}, where the two graders differ. No K$=5$ model state
has a repeatability replicate, so cross-grader agreement on this arm cannot be benchmarked against
a measured attenuation ceiling --- we report the raw correlations and say so.
