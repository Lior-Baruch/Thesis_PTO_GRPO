\section{Where the training grader stops discriminating}
\label{sec:measurement}

\S\ref{sec:reward} rests on two graders agreeing about an ordering. This section reports where, at
the level of individual conversations, they stop agreeing: at the winning checkpoint itself.

\textbf{Arm-level agreement is strong.} Across the 22 model states, the held-out judge preserves
the sign of $1{,}640$ of $8 \times \binom{22}{2} = 1{,}848$ pairwise state $\times$ instrument
contrasts ($88.7\%$); restricted to contrasts the training oracle scores at $|\Delta| \geq 0.25$
this rises to $97.0\%$, and at $|\Delta| \geq 0.50$ to $98.9\%$. The graders disagree mainly about
differences too small to claim.

\textbf{Per-conversation agreement collapses at the winning checkpoint.} That statistic is
arm-level and licenses no per-conversation claim. On the rewarded rubric Q1, per-conversation
agreement between the two graders along the $\Kf$ arm reads $0.941$ at iteration 5, then $0.877$,
$0.842$, $0.769$, and falls to $\mathbf{0.487}$ at iteration 9 and $\mathbf{0.544}$ at iteration
10, against a median of $0.841$ across the 22 states; the next-lowest state anywhere is $0.744$.
The best checkpoint by score is the checkpoint the two graders most disagree about, conversation
by conversation. The $\Kz$ arm never leaves the normal range on Q1 ($0.744$--$0.882$); with one
training run per arm we cannot separate ``this checkpoint'' from ``long $K{=}5$ training.''

Nor is the collapse confined to the rewarded rubric, though seeing that requires reading each
instrument against its own baseline, since the eight differ in baseline agreement by more than the
effect (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:agreement}). Ranked against their own 22-state distributions, the
MITI coder falls furthest ($-0.345$ from its median, its worst state in the experiment), then Q1
($-0.297$), Q2, and MICI; the four patient-perspective Likert instruments barely move. The graders
stop agreeing on the rewarded and behaviour-coding rubrics and keep agreeing on the
global-impression ones, consistent with saturation on what was optimised (below) stacking on the
known difficulty of cross-model behaviour coding: MITI is the least dependable instrument in the
battery (single-grader dependability $0.624$ against $0.91$--$0.97$ for the Likert rubrics), which
is also why \S\ref{sec:behaviour} rests on the deterministic marker rather than on coded counts.

\textbf{The mechanism is one-sided saturation, not homogenised output.} A correlation can collapse
because the conversations became uniform or because one rater ran out of range. Here it is the
second, and the two are distinguishable because only one grader's spread moves
(Figure~\ref{fig:saturation}). The training oracle's Q1 standard deviation falls monotonically
across $\Kf$'s run, from $1.336$ at base to $0.701$ at iteration 10 (Spearman $\rho = -0.86$,
$p = .001$), a variance ratio of $0.701^2/1.336^2 = 0.275$ that is robust to the anchor ($0.285$
against iteration 1). Its scores pile up near the top of the scale. On the \emph{identical}
conversations the held-out judge's Q1 spread does not move ($\rho = +0.44$, $p = .18$). Flat is
sufficient: had the conversations themselves become homogeneous, both spreads would have shrunk.
One shrank and one did not, on the same 96 conversations, so what changed is the ruler and not the
thing measured.

\textbf{What this does and does not undermine.} Not the headline: the held-out judge, the grader
still discriminating, ranks $\Kf$ above $\Kz$ at $\dz\,1.030$, a larger standardised effect than
the saturating grader reports. It does undermine three things, marked wherever they appear: any
per-conversation claim about $\Kf$'s late checkpoints on Q1, Q2, MITI, or MICI; the absolute
endpoint level $4.517$, which sits in the compressed part of the training oracle's range and means
``near the top of this grader's scale'' rather than a calibrated score; and the precise magnitude
of the residual MI-inconsistency in \S\ref{sec:behaviour-honest}. No $K{=}5$ state has an
oracle-repeatability replicate, so these correlations are raw, not attenuation-corrected.
