\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Task and simulator}

The therapist is \texttt{Llama-3.2-1B} (bf16, LoRA $r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}16$, lr $1\mathrm{e}{-}5$,
seed 42). The patient is \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} conditioned on one of \textbf{96} persona
system prompts, enumerated exhaustively as $2\ \text{gender} \times 3\ \text{cooperation} \times
2\ \text{problem} \times 2\ \text{problem duration} \times 2\ \text{prior attempts} \times
2\ \text{age} = 96$ with counsellor level fixed to expert. A session runs to a target of 49
utterances (therapist and patient combined), with a 200-token cap per response and a 2{,}048-token
therapist context; therapist decoding is at temperature 0.9 and the patient at 0.7.

\subsection{Reward}

The oracle is the same \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} model with JSON-schema-constrained output. The
\emph{training} reward is the mean of two validated MI questionnaires (Q1 session satisfaction and
Q2 working alliance; 22 Likert items in total), matching the look-ahead paper this work follows
\citep{baruch2025pto}. \emph{Evaluation} uses eight instruments: those two, WAI-SR, CSQ-8, MI-SAT
and a MITI-style coder, plus patient change talk (PCT) and MI-inconsistent behaviour (MICI, where
lower is better). Q1+Q2 is therefore both the optimisation target and a reported outcome; §\ref{sec:measurement}
and the Limitations treat that circularity explicitly, and it is the reason a second, held-out
grader is central rather than supplementary.

\subsection{The lever: what the judge is shown}
\label{sec:setup-lever}

GRPO samples $G{=}8$ completions per prompt, scores each, and forms a group-relative advantage
$A_g = (r_g - \operatorname{mean}_g r)/\operatorname{std}_g r$. The only thing we vary is the
transcript handed to the oracle when computing $r_g$. Writing $c$ for the conversation prefix and
$t_g$ for a candidate therapist turn:
\[
r_g =
\begin{cases}
O(c + t_g) & K = 0\\[2pt]
O\!\left(c + t_g + \underbrace{P + \pi_n + \cdots}_{K\ \text{further utterances}}\right) & K > 0
\end{cases}
\]
where $P$ is the patient simulator and $\pi_n$ the \emph{current} policy. At $K{=}5$ the patient
replies to the candidate, the policy replies to that, and so on for five further utterances; the
extended transcript is what the oracle scores (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}).

\subsection{What is matched}

The two arms are a controlled pair, and this is checkable rather than asserted: their tracked
\texttt{run\_metadata.json} configurations differ in \textbf{exactly two substantive fields}
($\texttt{lookahead\_k}$: none vs.\ 5, and its audit-mirror $\texttt{lookahead\_sub\_batch\_size}$),
with everything else --- $G{=}8$, KL $\beta{=}0.01$, GRPO temperature 1.2, batch $64 \times$
accumulation 2, learning rate, seed, minimum context length $\text{MCL}{=}12$, 96 conversations per
iteration, 2 epochs per iteration, 10 iterations, the same oracle and the same persona set ---
identical. The remaining differences are the arms' own names and output paths.

Both methods are \emph{iterative}: at each iteration the current policy generates 96 fresh
conversations against the 96 personas, those conversations are sliced after every patient turn whose
prefix has at least MCL utterances to form training prompts, the update is applied, the adapter is
swapped in, and the loop repeats. The conversations generated at the start of iteration $n$ are the
output of the iteration-$(n{-}1)$ policy, so they double as that policy's evaluation set; a
generate-only pass after the loop supplies matched evaluation data for the final adapter. This
yields 11 evaluated model states per arm (base plus iterations 1--10), or $2 \times 11 = 22$ for
this paper.

\subsection{Evaluation and statistics}

Every model state is scored on all eight instruments for all 96 personas by two graders: the
\textbf{training oracle} (\primaryjudge) and a \textbf{held-out judge} (\heldoutjudge), a different
model family that never played the patient and never touched training. All contrasts are
\textbf{persona-paired} --- the 96 personas are reshuffled every iteration, so pairing is on the
recovered persona identity, never on file order --- with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Cohen's \dz,
and 2{,}000-resample percentile bootstrap intervals at a fixed seed. Holm correction is applied
within each family of tests and is not pooled across families.

Two rules follow from the grader design and are observed throughout. First, the two graders'
\emph{levels} are not comparable (the held-out judge is systematically harsher by 1.2--1.7 points,
and the offset is model-dependent), so only contrasts are compared across graders and the two are
never averaged --- averaging an optimisation target with a held-out test is not averaging two
raters. Second, every behavioural claim names its denominator (per therapist turn, per session, or
as a share of coded acts), because the arms differ in both turn count and turn length.
