\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task, simulator, reward.} The therapist is \texttt{Llama-3.2-1B} (bf16, LoRA $r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}16$, lr $1\mathrm{e}{-}5$, seed 42). The patient is \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}
conditioned on one of \textbf{96} persona system prompts, enumerated exhaustively as
$2\ \text{gender} \times 3\ \text{cooperation} \times 2\ \text{problem} \times 2\ \text{duration}
\times 2\ \text{prior attempts} \times 2\ \text{age} = 96$. A session runs to a target of 49
utterances, with a 200-token cap per response and a 2{,}048-token therapist context; therapist
decoding is at temperature 0.9, the patient at 0.7 (full configuration in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The oracle is the same \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} with
JSON-schema-constrained output. The \emph{training} reward is the mean of two validated MI
questionnaires (Q1 session satisfaction and Q2 working alliance; 22 Likert items in total),
matching \citet{baruch2025pto}. \emph{Evaluation} uses eight instruments: those two, WAI-SR,
CSQ-8, MI-SAT, a MITI-style coder, patient change talk (PCT), and MI-inconsistent behaviour (MICI,
lower is better). Q1+Q2 is therefore both the optimisation target and a reported outcome;
\S\ref{sec:measurement} and Appendix~\ref{app:limitations} treat that circularity explicitly, and
it is why a second, held-out grader is central rather than supplementary.

\textbf{The lever: what the judge is shown.}
\label{sec:setup-lever}
GRPO samples $G{=}8$ completions per prompt, scores each, and forms a group-relative advantage
$A_g = (r_g - \operatorname{mean}_g r)/\operatorname{std}_g r$. The only thing we vary is the
transcript handed to the oracle when computing $r_g$. Writing $c$ for the conversation prefix and
$t_g$ for a candidate therapist turn:
\[
r_g =
\begin{cases}
O(c + t_g) & K = 0\\[2pt]
O\!\left(c + t_g + \underbrace{P + \pi_n + \cdots}_{K\ \text{further utterances}}\right) & K > 0
\end{cases}
\]
where $P$ is the patient simulator and $\pi_n$ the \emph{current} policy: at $K{=}5$ the patient
replies to the candidate, the policy replies to that, and so on for five further utterances, and
the extended transcript is what the oracle scores (schematic in Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}). The loss, the advantage definition, and the KL penalty are untouched
--- $K$ changes only the evidence on which a candidate is ranked against its seven siblings.

\textbf{What is matched.} The two arms are a controlled pair, and this is checkable rather than
asserted: their tracked \texttt{run\_metadata.json} configurations differ in \textbf{exactly two
substantive fields} ($\texttt{lookahead\_k}$ and its audit-mirror
$\texttt{lookahead\_sub\_batch\_size}$); everything else --- $G{=}8$, KL $\beta{=}0.01$, GRPO
temperature 1.2, batch $64 \times 2$ accumulation, learning rate, seed, minimum context length
$\text{MCL}{=}12$, 96 conversations per iteration, 2 epochs per iteration, 10 iterations, oracle,
persona set --- is identical. Training is \emph{iterative}: each iteration the current policy
generates 96 fresh conversations, they are sliced after every patient turn whose prefix has at
least MCL utterances to form training prompts, the update is applied, and the loop repeats. The
conversations generated at the start of iteration $n$ are the output of the iteration-$(n{-}1)$
policy and double as its evaluation set; a generate-only pass after the loop covers the final
adapter, yielding 11 evaluated model states per arm (base plus iterations 1--10).

\textbf{Evaluation and statistics.} Every model state is scored on all eight instruments for all
96 personas by two graders: the \textbf{training oracle} (\primaryjudge) and a \textbf{held-out
judge} (\heldoutjudge), a different model family that never played the patient and never touched
training. All contrasts are \textbf{persona-paired} --- personas are reshuffled every iteration,
so pairing is on the recovered persona identity, never file order --- with Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests, Cohen's \dz, and 2{,}000-resample percentile bootstrap intervals at a fixed seed; Holm
correction is applied within each family of tests. Two rules follow from the grader design and hold
throughout: the graders' \emph{levels} are not comparable (the held-out judge is harsher by
1.2--1.7 points, model-dependently), so only contrasts cross graders and the two are never
averaged --- an optimisation target and a held-out test are not two raters; and every behavioural
claim names its denominator (per turn, per session, or share of coded acts), because the arms
differ in both turn count and turn length.
