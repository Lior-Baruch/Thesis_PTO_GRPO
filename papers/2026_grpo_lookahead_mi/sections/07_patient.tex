\textbf{What each behaviour yields.} Figure~\ref{fig:process}c reads the coded pairs the other
way round: for each therapist code, how often the patient's next utterance is change talk. A
complex reflection by the $\Kf$ endpoint policy is followed by change talk in $85\%$ of cases
under the training oracle ($389$ turns) and $89\%$ under the held-out judge ($431$ turns); the
$\Kz$ policy's rare complex reflections are followed by change talk in $10\%$ / $9\%$ ($21$ /
$23$ turns). Praise is not unproductive in itself: at base it is followed by change talk $97\%$ /
$72\%$ of the time, because the base policy praises a patient who has just said something
hopeful. What the $\Kz$ policy learns is to praise \emph{regardless}, and its praise yields change
talk in $58\%$ / $49\%$ of cases ($467$ / $813$ turns), no better than its plain
information-giving turns, while the $\Kf$ policy's rarer praise keeps the base's yield ($76\%$ /
$97\%$), for the reason the next paragraph gives.

\textbf{Responsiveness: what the therapist does next.} The clearest MI-process contrast in the
experiment is the middle block of Table~\ref{tab:process}. When the patient produces change talk,
the $\Kf$ endpoint policy reflects it in $26\%$ of cases under both graders and the $\Kz$ policy
in $0.3\%$ / $0.6\%$, against $4$--$15\%$ at base (Holm-significant at six / five of the ten
iterations). When the patient produces sustain talk, the $\Kz$ policy answers with praise in
$32\%$ / $72\%$ of cases and the $\Kf$ policy in $1.5\%$ / $1.1\%$, against at most $3\%$ at
base (Holm-significant at iterations 8--10 / 5--10; trajectories in Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:responsiveness}). This is Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} as a rate: the turn-level
policy meets resistance with reassurance, which the rubric rewards in the turn where it happens;
the look-ahead policy meets the patient's own case for change with a reflection, which pays in
the turns the rubric is not shown at $K{=}0$.

\textbf{The session as a whole.} The patient's change-talk share is higher under look-ahead at
the endpoint ($0.68$ vs.\ $0.53$ under the training oracle, $0.72$ vs.\ $0.55$ held out;
$\dz\,0.57$ / $0.70$) and at every iteration from the sixth / fourth on, while the share of
sessions that reach any change talk at all does not differ ($0.92$ vs.\ $0.87$, n.s.). The
difference is in how a session develops (Figure~\ref{fig:process}d): both endpoint policies raise
the patient's change talk faster than the base over the first nine patient turns, but from the
tenth on the $\Kf$ sessions keep rising, to a change-talk share of $0.85$ / $0.88$, where the
$\Kz$ sessions fall back to $0.50$ / $0.50$ from $0.68$ / $0.62$, below the base: the praise
spiral of Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} at scale. The $\Kf$ policy's patients also write longer turns
($600$ vs.\ $440$ characters, $\dz\,1.24$); a crude disengagement cue (``I don't know'', ``waste
of time'') is slightly \emph{more} frequent in their turns ($\dz\,0.31$), which we report rather
than resolve.
