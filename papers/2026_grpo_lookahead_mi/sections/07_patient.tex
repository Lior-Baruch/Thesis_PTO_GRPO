\section{What the therapist's turns do to the patient}
\label{sec:patient}

\textbf{How the therapist replies.} 
% \dnotedone{endpoint dropped; the opening says iteration 10}{[endpoint ?]}
% \dnotedone{answered: reflection defined in §3.1, reply rates in Appendix D.3}{[reflects it?]}
% \dnotedone{metaphor dropped: now change talk, defined in §3.1}{[own case for change -?]}
When the patient expresses change talk, the $\Kf$ policy
reflects it in $0.26$ of cases, against $0.003$ for the $\Kz$ policy (Base $0.14$;
Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:responsiveness}), a difference significant at iterations 1 and 6--10. When
the patient expresses sustain talk, MI's answer is again a reflection, and the two policies do not
differ significantly in how often they give it ($0.37$ under $\Kf$, $0.26$ under $\Kz$, $0.22$ at
the Base). Instead, each has its own MI-inconsistent answer. 
% \dnotedone{rephrased: praise regardless became praise in reply to sustain talk}{of what?}
% \dnotedone{rephrased: the reply of Table 2, measured across conversations}{[This is Table \ref{tab:excerpt} as a rate -?]}
% \dnotedone{renamed: K=0 / K=5 only in the results}{[turn-level policy - 0? use consistent names]}
The $\Kz$ policy praises: $0.32$ of
its replies to sustain talk, against $0.02$ at the Base: the kind of reply
Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} shows, measured across conversations. The $\Kf$ policy persuades: $0.39$ of
its replies to sustain talk, against $0.24$ at the Base, which is MI's righting reflex. 
% \dnotedone{answered: praise premium measured, Appendix C.4}{[which the rubric rewards in the turn where it happens ??]}
Both runs' rewards gave
higher scores to praise in the middle of training: among the eight candidate replies the reward
compares for a prompt, those containing a keyword-marker phrase scored $0.22$--$0.33$ within-group
standard deviations above the candidates that did not praise under $\Kz$ and $0.16$--$0.21$ under
$\Kf$ (candidates sampled from the models of iterations 4--7), but for candidates from the
iteration-9 model only $\Kz$'s reward still did ($0.16$ against $0.04$; Appendix~\ref{app:premium},
Figure~\ref{fig:premium}).

% \dnotedone{cut; measured change-talk persistence replaces it}{[ pays in the turns the rubric is not shown at $K{=}0$. ?]}
\textbf{Whether change talk continues.} Under the $\Kf$ policy, a patient who has just expressed
change talk expresses it again in the next utterance in $0.97$ of cases, against $0.80$ under $\Kz$
(Base $0.75$; Figure~\ref{fig:process}c), a difference significant at every iteration
from 5 on. No single kind of reply accounts for it: the $\Kf$ policy's four most common replies to
change talk (complex reflections, affirmations, persuasion and information giving) are each
followed by change talk in at least $97\%$ of cases, pooled over turns. 
% \dnotedone{cut: reading by therapist code retired; reason given here}{[the other way round: ?]}
% \dnotedone{cut with the praise-yield paragraph}{[for the reason the next paragraph gives. \textasciitilde{}]}
We therefore do
not read the change talk that follows each therapist code as that code's effect: $83\%$ of the
$\Kf$ policy's complex reflections come right after change talk, so the change talk after them is
mostly the patient continuing, and once the patient's previous utterance is held fixed,
reflections do about as well as the policy's other replies ($0.99$ against $0.99$ after change
talk, $0.16$ against $0.17$ after sustain talk). Turning sustain talk around is harder and differs less:
after sustain talk the next utterance is change talk in $0.33$ of cases under $\Kf$ and $0.28$
under $\Kz$ (Base $0.28$), a difference significant at iterations 5--7 and 9 but not at 10.

\textbf{The session as a whole.} 
% \dnotedone{rephrased: iteration 10, one judge, each run named}{[the endpoint ($0.68$ vs.\ $0.53$ under the training oracle, $0.72$ vs.\ $0.55$ held out; $\dz\,0.57$ / $0.70$) - not clear]}
At iteration 10, $0.68$ of the patient's utterances are change
talk under $\Kf$ against $0.53$ under $\Kz$ (Base $0.45$), and the difference is significant at
every iteration from 6 on, while the share of sessions that reach any change talk at all does not
differ ($0.92$ vs.\ $0.86$). 
% \dnotedone{rephrased: the difference between the runs}{between waht}
The difference between the runs is in how a session develops
(Figure~\ref{fig:process}d). 
% \dnotedone{cut: the praise-spiral clause dropped}{[at scale ?]}
Over the first nine patient turns both final policies raise change
talk above the Base; in the sessions that reach a tenth patient turn (74 of $\Kf$'s 96, 62 of
$\Kz$'s), change talk keeps rising under $\Kf$, to $0.85$ of the patient's utterances from the
tenth on, and falls back under $\Kz$, to $0.50$, below the Base's $0.58$. Two further differences
on the patient's side have no single reading: the $\Kf$ policy's patients write longer utterances
($600$ vs.\ $440$ characters, $\dz\,1.24$), and a crude keyword cue for disengagement (``I don't
know'', ``waste of time'') is slightly more frequent in them ($\dz\,0.31$). 
% \dnotedone{rephrased: why neither is interpreted is now stated}{[rather than resolve. ?]}
With a simulated
patient we cannot tell engagement from verbosity, so we note both without interpreting either.

The held-out judge agrees on the direction of each contrast at iteration 10, and its paired effect
is the larger on ten of the twelve measures (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:process-heldout}): under it the
$\Kf$ policy persuades after $0.58$ of the patient's sustain talk (Base $0.25$), and it reflects
sustain talk significantly more often than $\Kz$ ($0.17$ vs.\ $0.05$).
