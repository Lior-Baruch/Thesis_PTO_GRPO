\label{sec:patient}

\textbf{How the therapist replies.}

When the patient expresses change talk, the $\Kf$ policy answers with a reflection in $26\%$ of
cases, against $0.3\%$ under $\Kz$ (Base $14\%$; Figure~\ref{fig:responsiveness}). A reflection is also
one of MI's answers to sustain talk \citep{miller2013mi}, and the $\Kf$ policy gives it somewhat
more often ($37\%$, against $26\%$ under $\Kz$ and $22\%$ at the Base); the difference is
significant against $\Kz$'s best checkpoint but not its last. Each final policy also has
its own MI-inconsistent answer to sustain talk.

The $\Kz$ policy praises: $32\%$ of its replies to sustain talk, against $2\%$ at the Base. The
$\Kf$ policy persuades: $39\%$ of its replies to sustain talk, against $24\%$ at the Base.

\textbf{Whether change talk continues.} In the average conversation under the $\Kf$ policy, a
patient who has just expressed change talk expresses it again in their next utterance in $97\%$ of
cases, against $80\%$ under $\Kz$ ($88\%$ at its best checkpoint; Base $75\%$;
Figure~\ref{fig:process}e).

No single kind of reply explains the gap: after change talk, each of the $\Kf$ policy's four most
common replies, complex reflections included, is followed by change talk about as often as the
others (Appendix~\ref{app:reply}). After sustain talk, the next patient utterance is change talk
in $33\%$ of cases under $\Kf$, against $28\%$ under $\Kz$ and at the Base, a difference significant
against $\Kz$'s best checkpoint ($22\%$) but not against its last.

The $\Kf$ policy's patients also write longer utterances and slightly more often use phrases such
as ``I don't know'' that a keyword cue counts as disengagement (Appendix~\ref{app:patientside}), so we
do not read their length as engagement.

The held-out judge agrees on the direction of every contrast at iteration 10
(Table~\ref{tab:process-heldout}): under it the $\Kf$ policy persuades after $58\%$ of the patient's
sustain talk (Base $25\%$), and change talk is followed by change talk in $94\%$ of cases under
$\Kf$, against $73\%$ under $\Kz$ (Base $71\%$).
