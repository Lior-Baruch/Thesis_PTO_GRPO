\section{What the therapist's turns do to the patient}
\label{sec:patient}

MI's own test of a therapist behaviour is what the patient says next. The utterance coder labels
every utterance in transcript order, so each patient utterance can be followed through the
therapist's reply to the patient's next utterance. Table~\ref{tab:process} collects the measures at
iteration 10.

\textbf{How the therapist replies.} When the patient voices change talk, the $\Kf$ policy
reflects it in $0.26$ of cases, against $0.003$ for the $\Kz$ policy and $0.14$ at the Base, a
difference significant at iterations 1 and 6--10 (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:responsiveness}). When
the patient voices sustain talk, MI's answer is again a reflection, and the two policies do not
differ significantly in how often they give it ($0.37$ under $\Kf$, $0.26$ under $\Kz$, $0.22$ at
the Base). Instead, each has its own MI-inconsistent answer. The $\Kz$ policy praises: $0.32$ of
its replies to sustain talk, against $0.02$ at the Base, which is the reply of
Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} measured across conversations. The $\Kf$ policy persuades: $0.39$ of
its replies, against $0.24$ at the Base, which is MI's righting reflex. Both runs' rewards paid for
praise in the middle of training: among the eight candidate replies the reward compares, those
containing a keyword-marker phrase scored above their siblings by $0.22$--$0.33$ within-group
standard deviations under $\Kz$ and $0.16$--$0.21$ under $\Kf$ (candidates sampled from the models
of iterations 4--7), but by iteration 9 only $\Kz$'s reward still did ($0.16$ against $0.04$;
Appendix~\ref{app:premium}).

\textbf{Whether change talk continues.} Under the $\Kf$ policy, a patient who has just voiced
change talk voices it again in the next utterance in $0.97$ of cases, against $0.80$ under $\Kz$
and $0.75$ at the Base (Figure~\ref{fig:process}c), a difference significant at every iteration
from 5 on. No single kind of reply accounts for it: the $\Kf$ policy's four most common replies to
change talk (complex reflections, affirmations, persuasion and information giving) are each
followed by change talk in at least $97\%$ of cases, pooled over turns. For the same reason we do
not read the change talk that follows each therapist code as that code's effect: $83\%$ of the
$\Kf$ policy's complex reflections come right after change talk, so the change talk after them is
mostly the patient continuing, and once the patient's previous utterance is held fixed,
reflections do about as well as the policy's other replies ($0.99$ against $0.99$ after change
talk, $0.16$ against $0.17$ after sustain talk). Turning sustain talk around is harder and differs less:
after sustain talk the next utterance is change talk in $0.33$ of cases under $\Kf$ and $0.28$
under $\Kz$ (Base $0.28$), a difference significant at iterations 5--7 and 9 but not at 10.

\textbf{The session as a whole.} At iteration 10, $0.68$ of the patient's utterances are change
talk under $\Kf$ against $0.53$ under $\Kz$ (Base $0.45$), and the difference is significant at
every iteration from 6 on, while the share of sessions that reach any change talk at all does not
differ ($0.92$ vs.\ $0.86$). The difference between the runs is in how a session develops
(Figure~\ref{fig:process}d). Over the first nine patient turns both final policies raise change
talk above the Base; in the sessions that reach a tenth patient turn (74 of $\Kf$'s 96, 62 of
$\Kz$'s), change talk keeps rising under $\Kf$, to $0.85$ of the patient's utterances from the
tenth on, and falls back under $\Kz$, to $0.50$, below the Base's $0.58$. Two further differences
on the patient's side have no single reading: the $\Kf$ policy's patients write longer utterances
($600$ vs.\ $440$ characters, $\dz\,1.24$), and a crude keyword cue for disengagement (``I don't
know'', ``waste of time'') is slightly more frequent in them ($\dz\,0.31$). With a simulated
patient we cannot tell engagement from verbosity, so we note both without interpreting either.

The held-out judge agrees on the direction of each contrast at iteration 10 and finds two of them
larger: the $\Kf$ policy persuades after $0.58$ of the patient's sustain talk (Base $0.25$), and
it reflects sustain talk significantly more often than $\Kz$ ($0.17$ vs.\ $0.05$; Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:process-heldout}).
