\section*{Ethics Statement}

\textbf{No human subjects, and no clinical claim.} Every conversation in this work is between two
language models. No human patient data was collected, used, or generated, and no human participants
were involved at any stage. Nothing here is evidence that the resulting model is safe or effective
for use with people. A 1B-parameter model trained against a simulated patient and graded by another
language model is a research artifact; motivational interviewing is a clinical skill practised with
people in distress, and no number in this paper bridges that gap.

\textbf{The failure mode we document is safety-relevant.} The $K{=}0$ policy learned to praise its
patient more or less regardless of what they said, and its score went \emph{up} while it did so. In
a counselling setting that is not a neutral artifact of optimisation: unearned affirmation of
ambivalence or of a harmful plan is precisely the error MI trains counsellors out of. We report it
prominently, including the residual drift that survives under look-ahead
(\S\ref{sec:behaviour-honest}), so that a reader evaluating this line of work sees the failure mode
as clearly as the improvement.

\textbf{Simulated patients encode a narrow population.} The 96 personas vary six attributes around
two presenting problems (smoking and obesity). They are a coarse and culturally narrow caricature
of help-seeking, authored by the researchers rather than derived from clinical data, and results on
them should not be read as generalising across presenting problems, demographics, or cultures. The
saturated design means we cannot measure generalisation even within this grid.

\textbf{Judges inherit their models' biases.} Both graders are commercial LLMs whose training data
and alignment procedures are undisclosed. They disagree systematically about level and, as
\S\ref{sec:measurement} shows, can disagree severely about individual conversations. Using such
models as the sole arbiter of therapeutic quality is a methodological convenience, not a validated
measurement, and any downstream use of this reward signal should treat it as such.

\textbf{Compute and models.} Training used a single cloud A100-class GPU for approximately 79
GPU-hours in total across the two arms (reconstructed from artifact timestamps;
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), plus API inference for the patient simulator and the graders. The base
model is openly licensed; the patient simulator and graders are commercial APIs used within their
terms of service.
