\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One training run per arm.}
Every effect size reported here is computed across the 96 patient personas \emph{within} a single
training run. There are no training-seed replicates, so run-to-run training variance is entirely
unmeasured, and we cannot separate ``look-ahead helps'' from ``this look-ahead run went well.''
This is the limitation we would most like to remove and the one a reader should weight most
heavily. It is distinct from, and not bounded by, the evaluation noise floor below.

\paragraph{Evaluation draws: replicated at the endpoint, single elsewhere.}
Therapist decoding is unseeded, and each model state was evaluated on one 96-conversation sample.
Two noise floors bound this. At the untrained base, four independent draws of the identical policy
yield 54 same-policy contrasts with none reaching even uncorrected $p < .05$ --- but that is
measured where sessions are short and homogeneous. At the \emph{trained} endpoint we re-drew the
winning state (\S\ref{sec:reward}): 9 instruments $\times$ 2 graders of the same policy differ
by at most $|\dz|\,0.174$, nothing significant, and the $K$ contrast keeps its sign, significance
and effect size. The remaining model states are still single draws, and a replicate bounds
\emph{evaluation} noise only --- never the run-to-run training variance above.

\paragraph{Matched iterations are not matched cost.}
Every comparison in this paper is made at matched iteration counts, and an iteration is 96
conversations and $G{=}8$ scored candidates per prompt in both arms --- oracle scoring calls are
approximately matched by construction ($302{,}541$ for $\Kz$ vs.\ $289{,}983$ for $\Kf$ over the
ten iterations). But each $\Kf$ reward requires a five-utterance simulated continuation --- one
patient-simulator call and one policy generation per utterance, ${\approx}393$k patient calls over
the run that the $\Kz$ arm never makes --- and roughly doubles per-optimizer-step wall-clock
(median $1.92\times$). ``More per iteration'' is built into the lever; we disclose the asymmetry
rather than equalise it, and nothing in this paper compares the two arms at matched cost.

\paragraph{$K \in \{0, 5\}$ only.}
$K$ was run at two levels by design. There is no dose--response evidence between them and nothing
above 5, so nothing here says the effect is monotone in $K$, that 5 is optimal, or that a shorter
horizon would not capture most of the benefit. An intermediate $K$ is the most valuable single
follow-up: it would locate how much of the benefit a shorter --- and cheaper --- horizon captures.

\paragraph{The patient distribution is saturated, so every number is in-sample.}
The 96 personas are the exhaustive enumeration of the persona grid, and the self-training loop uses
those same 96 as both the training rollouts and the evaluation set at every iteration.
Generalisation to unseen patients is therefore not merely unmeasured but unmeasurable without
authoring new persona prompts.

\paragraph{The grader is decoupled; the generator is not.}
The held-out judge addresses the concern that the patient simulator and the training oracle are the
same model, but only for the \emph{grading} half. Every conversation in this paper was generated
against a \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} patient. A fully decoupled replication would require regenerating
all conversations with a patient from another model family.

\paragraph{No human validation.}
All eight instruments are administered by language models. The oracle is highly self-repeatable
(ICC 0.86--0.99 on the measured subset), but repeatability is not validity: a judge can be
perfectly consistent and consistently wrong. No human MI coder has rated any conversation in this
experiment, and no ICC substitutes for that. The over-praise finding is the most robust behavioural
claim here precisely because a deterministic lexical marker corroborates it without a model in the
loop.

\paragraph{The reward is also a reported outcome.}
Q1+Q2 is both the optimisation target and a headline metric, so ``Q1+Q2 improved'' is partly
circular. We treat the held-out judge and the instruments outside the reward (MICI, PCT, the MITI
technique coder) as the load-bearing evidence, and \S\ref{sec:measurement} quantifies what happens
to the trained-against grader's discriminative range over a long run.

\paragraph{Instrument-specific reliability.}
The MITI-style coder is the least dependable instrument in the battery at the arm level, and we do
not lean on its differences alone. There is no reliability estimate for any \emph{individual}
MI-inconsistency channel --- including over-praise --- which is the granularity
\S\ref{sec:behaviour} argues at; this is the main reason that section's conclusion rests on the
deterministic marker.
