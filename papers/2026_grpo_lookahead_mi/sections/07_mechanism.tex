\section{Why it works, as far as we can tell}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Three mechanisms were testable in this design, and none is confirmed
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism} has the full analysis). \emph{A more faithful reward?} The training
reward scores a partial conversation while the evaluation scores the whole session. Pooled over
the run, the $\Kf$ signal ranks candidates more like the full-session score does (pairwise rank
agreement $0.909$ vs.\ $0.873$ under the training oracle, $0.800$ vs.\ $0.747$ held out), but the
iteration-level test does not clear $.05$ ($p = .084$ / $.193$), and at the one matched-policy
cut, iteration 1 with both arms branching from the same base, both deltas straddle zero ($+0.015$
$[-0.026, 0.059]$; $-0.014$ $[-0.075, 0.048]$): unsupported. \emph{Sharper group
discrimination?} Look-ahead widens the best-minus-worst margin within a group ($1.300\times$), but
the within-group standard deviation grows by almost the same factor ($1.293\times$) and GRPO
divides by it (ratio of ratios $1.006$ $[1.002, 1.010]$): a rescaling the advantage normalisation
absorbs, so look-ahead changes \emph{which} candidate the group prefers, not how confidently.
\emph{A different update direction?} The two arms' advantage-weighted sentence-embedding directions
have a cosine of $0.804$ ($0.851$ after correcting for the split-half reliability ceiling): barely.

What remains is that the same optimiser, pushed in nearly the same direction by a reward that is
not measurably better conditioned or more faithful at a fixed policy, ends more than twice as far
from base. What differs is which of eight siblings is preferred at each step, a small, repeated
difference in selection compounded over ten iterations of a loop that regenerates its own training
data from the policy it just updated. Isolating that pathway needs the horizon swapped mid-run on
one policy, a cut this design lacks.
