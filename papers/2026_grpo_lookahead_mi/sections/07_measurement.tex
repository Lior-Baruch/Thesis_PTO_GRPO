\section{Saturation of the training oracle at the winning checkpoint}
\label{sec:measurement}

\S\ref{sec:reward} rests on two graders agreeing about an ordering, and at the arm level they do:
across the 22 model states, the held-out judge preserves the sign of $1{,}640$ of
$8 \times \binom{22}{2} = 1{,}848$ pairwise state $\times$ instrument contrasts ($88.7\%$), and
$98.9\%$ of those the training oracle scores at $|\Delta| \geq 0.50$. At the level of individual
conversations they stop agreeing at the winning checkpoint.

\textbf{Per-conversation agreement collapses at the winning checkpoint.} On the rewarded rubric
Q1, per-conversation agreement between the two graders along the $\Kf$ arm reads $0.941$ at
iteration 5, then $0.877$, $0.842$, $0.769$, and falls to $\mathbf{0.487}$ at iteration 9 and
$\mathbf{0.544}$ at iteration 10, against a median of $0.842$ across the 22 states; the next-lowest
state anywhere is $0.744$. The $\Kz$ arm never leaves the normal
range on Q1 ($0.744$--$0.882$). The collapse is not confined to the rewarded rubric: against each
instrument's own 22-state distribution, MITI falls furthest, then Q1, Q2 and MICI, while the four
patient-perspective Likert instruments barely move (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:agreement}). With one
training run per arm we cannot separate ``this checkpoint'' from ``long $K{=}5$ training.''

\textbf{The mechanism is a ceiling, and only one grader is at it.} A correlation can collapse
because the conversations became uniform or because one rater ran out of range. Here only one
grader's spread moves. Along the $\Kf$ arm the
training oracle's Q1 scores climb into the top of its 1--5 scale: the share of conversations at
$4.5$ or above rises from $14\%$ at base to $58\%$ at iteration 10, where $40\%$ receive the
maximum score, and the per-conversation standard deviation falls
from $1.336$ to $0.701$ (Spearman $\rho = -0.86$, $p = .001$; variance ratio
$0.701^2/1.336^2 = 0.275$, or $0.285$ against iteration 1). The same grader's spread along the
$\Kz$ arm tracks that arm's level rather than its horizon: it compresses to $0.92$--$1.01$ while
the mean sits near $3.9$ over iterations 3--8 and re-expands to $1.14$ once the mean has fallen to
$3.6$ over iterations 9--10. On the \emph{identical} $\Kf$ conversations the held-out judge, whose
mean sits near $2.7$ with the upper half of its scale unused, keeps its spread ($\rho = +0.44$,
$p = .18$). Had the conversations become homogeneous, both spreads would have shrunk; only the
grader optimised against did.

\textbf{What this does and does not undermine.} Not the headline: the held-out judge, still
discriminating, ranks $\Kf$ above $\Kz$ at $\dz\,1.030$, a larger effect than the saturating
oracle reports. It does undermine per-conversation claims about $\Kf$'s late checkpoints on Q1,
Q2, MITI, or MICI; the absolute endpoint level $4.517$ in the compressed top of the training
oracle's range; and the precise magnitude of the residual MI-inconsistency in
\S\ref{sec:behaviour-heldout}.
