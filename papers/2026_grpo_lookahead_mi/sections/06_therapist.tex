In the clearest case, the first reply to a reluctant 61-year-old smoker sent to therapy (her first
utterance differs between the runs), the $\Kz$ policy answers ``Great to hear that you're ready to
try again. That's very courageous [\ldots] I'm so proud of you for taking this step'', praising a
readiness she has not expressed. The $\Kf$ policy answers ``You're right, it's just a habit [\ldots]
It's not really because you don't want to stop'', agreeing that it is a habit and inferring that
she wants to stop before turning to advice and a question; both judges' coders label them
non-specific praise and complex reflection, respectively. The pair ranks first of 28 such pairs on
lexical features (Appendix~\ref{app:example-clear}; a pair picked from eight personas drawn at
random: Appendix~\ref{app:example-drawn}).

\textbf{The two policies learn different behaviors.} The Base's turns are mostly giving
information and persuasion, followed by simple reflections and open questions, with no other code
above $0.05$ of turns (Table~\ref{tab:codes}).

Under $\Kz$ the distribution of therapist behaviors shifts, late and unevenly, toward
\textbf{non-specific praise}: from $0.04$ of the Base's turns to $0.22$ of the $\Kz$ policy's at
iteration 8, $0.08$ at iteration 9 and $0.41$ at iteration 10 (Figure~\ref{fig:process}a),
significantly more than under $\Kf$ at iterations 8 and 10. The dip at iteration 9 follows the one
late round, on the iteration-8 model's candidates, in which the $\Kz$ reward barely favored praise
(Appendix~\ref{app:premium}).
% \dnotedone{reworded: now `Under K=5 it' (the behaviour distribution)}{[the mix \textasciitilde{}]}
Under $\Kf$ the shift is instead toward \textbf{complex reflections} (Figure~\ref{fig:process}c): from $0.02$ of the Base's
turns to $0.23$ at iteration 10, significantly more than under $\Kz$ at every iteration from 4 on,
and the share of MI-consistent turns at iteration 10 is $0.44$ against $\Kz$'s $0.29$ (Base
$0.31$). Both policies lose open-question turns ($0.09$ of the Base's turns; $\Kz$ $0.00$, $\Kf$ $0.01$)
and roughly triple their turn length ($273$ characters at the Base; $\Kz$ $896$, $\Kf$ $849$), near
the 200-token cap.

The $\Kf$ policy also persuades more: persuasion, arguing for change on the patient's behalf (the
\emph{righting reflex} that MI warns against; \citealp{miller2013mi}), rises from $0.20$ of the
Base's turns to $0.28$ of its own (Figure~\ref{fig:process}d).
% \dnotedone{rephrased: says what Chiu et al. report}{[the failure mode \citet{chiu2024bolt} report for LLM therapists -?]}
It is the tendency \citet{chiu2024bolt} report in LLM therapists, which advise where an MI
counselor would reflect. The coder's MI-inconsistent share still ends lower under $\Kf$ ($0.33$ against
$0.46$; Base $0.25$; Table~\ref{tab:process}) because of the $\Kz$ policy's late praise; the $\Kf$
policy's own share rises above the Base's with its persuasion, though its MICI under the training
oracle does not (\S\ref{sec:reward}). The held-out judge agrees on the
direction of each coded change at iteration 10 and codes $0.76$ of the $\Kz$ policy's turns as praise
against $0.20$ of the $\Kf$ policy's, itself above the Base's $0.04$ (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}).

\textbf{Corroboration.} The praise finding could in principle be a judge artifact, but a
\textbf{keyword marker} of ten fixed praise phrases (``I'm so proud'', ``you got this'', \ldots;
Appendix~\ref{app:marker}), which involves no model, follows the coder's praise share over
iterations (Figure~\ref{fig:process}b; only its trend is read): almost none of the Base's turns,
low under $\Kf$, and under $\Kz$ a jump at iteration 8, a fall at 9 and a peak at 10. MICI agrees:
$8.3$ of the $9.9$ MI-inconsistent acts per session it counts for the $\Kz$ policy at iteration 10
fall under its over-praise code, which we added to the MISC's codes (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

In sentence-embedding space the $\Kz$ policy's replies vary less from patient to patient than the
$\Kf$ policy's, relative to their variation within a session, from iteration 3 on, long before the $\Kz$
praise peaks (Appendix~\ref{app:embedding}); and the embedding direction each round of $\Kz$
training favored points toward praise sentences at training iterations 4--8 and 10 in all five
encoders, further than the $\Kf$ direction in at least four of the five at 3--6
(Appendix~\ref{app:direction}). The praise finding thus rests on two judges' coders, MICI and a
marker that involves no model.
