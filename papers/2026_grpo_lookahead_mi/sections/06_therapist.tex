\textbf{The two policies learn different behaviors.} The $\Kz$ policy learned to praise and the
$\Kf$ policy to give complex reflections. The Base's turns are mostly giving
information and persuasion, followed by simple reflections and open questions, with no other code
above $5\%$ of turns (Table~\ref{tab:codes}).

Under $\Kz$ the distribution of therapist behaviors shifts, late and unevenly, toward
\textbf{non-specific praise}: from $4\%$ of the Base's turns to $22\%$ of the $\Kz$ policy's at
iteration 8, $8\%$ at iteration 9 and $41\%$ at iteration 10 (Figure~\ref{fig:process}a),
significantly more than under $\Kf$ at iterations 8 and 10. The dip at iteration 9 follows the one
late round, on the iteration-8 model's candidates, in which the $\Kz$ reward barely favored praise
(Appendix~\ref{app:premium}).

Under $\Kf$ the shift is instead toward \textbf{complex reflections} (Figure~\ref{fig:process}c):
from $2\%$ of the Base's turns to $23\%$ at iteration 10, significantly more than under $\Kz$ (never
above $3\%$) from iteration 4 on. At iteration 10, $44\%$ of the $\Kf$ policy's turns are
MI-consistent, against $29\%$ of the $\Kz$ policy's and $31\%$ of the Base's. Both policies nearly
stop asking open questions ($9\%$ of the Base's turns) and roughly triple the length of their turns,
from $273$ characters at the Base to $896$ under $\Kz$ and $849$ under $\Kf$, near the 200-token cap. In sentence-embedding space the $\Kz$ policy's replies vary less from patient to
patient than the $\Kf$ policy's, relative to their variation within a session, from iteration 3 on
(Appendix~\ref{app:embedding}).

The $\Kf$ policy also persuades more: persuasion, arguing for change on the patient's behalf (the
\emph{righting reflex} that MI warns against; \citealp{miller2013mi}), rises from $20\%$ of the
Base's turns to $28\%$ of its own (Figure~\ref{fig:process}d).
% \dnotedone{rephrased: says what Chiu et al. report}{[the failure mode \citet{chiu2024bolt} report for LLM therapists -?]}
It is the tendency \citet{chiu2024bolt} report in LLM therapists, which advise where an MI
counselor would reflect.

By the coder's count, $25\%$ of the Base's turns are MI-inconsistent, against $33\%$ of the $\Kf$
policy's, mostly persuasion, and $46\%$ of the $\Kz$ policy's, mostly praise (Table~\ref{tab:process}).
Yet the training oracle's MICI for the $\Kf$ policy does not rise (\S\ref{sec:reward}). The held-out
judge agrees on the direction of each coded change at iteration 10. It labels $76\%$ of the $\Kz$
policy's turns as praise and $20\%$ of the $\Kf$ policy's, both above the Base's $4\%$
(Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}).

\textbf{Corroboration.} A
\textbf{keyword marker} of ten fixed praise phrases (``I'm so proud'', ``you got this'', \ldots;
Appendix~\ref{app:marker}), which involves no model, follows the coder's praise share over
iterations (Figure~\ref{fig:process}b; only its trend is read): almost none of the Base's turns,
low under $\Kf$, and under $\Kz$ a jump at iteration 8, a fall at 9 and a peak at 10. MICI agrees:
$8.3$ of the $9.9$ MI-inconsistent acts per session it counts for the $\Kz$ policy at iteration 10
fall under its over-praise code, which we added to the MISC's codes (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
