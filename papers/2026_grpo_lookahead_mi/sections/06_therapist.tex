\section{What look-ahead teaches the therapist}
\label{sec:therapist}

A higher score is not by itself evidence of better therapy. This section and the next ask what the
two final policies do differently, mostly with the utterance coder (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

The clearest single case is each policy's first reply to the same reluctant patient, a
61-year-old smoker sent to therapy (Appendix~\ref{app:example-clear}). The $\Kz$ policy answers
``Great to hear that you're ready to try again. That's very courageous [\ldots] I'm so proud of you
for taking this step'', praising a step she has not said she is taking; the $\Kf$ policy answers
``You're right, it's just a habit. It's been a lifelong struggle for you'', agreeing that it is a
habit and reflecting her failed attempts, then turns to advice and a question. Both judges' coders
label the first reply non-specific praise and the second a complex reflection; the pair was chosen
from the top of a ranking of such pairs, so it is an illustration, not a typical exchange (a pair
drawn at random: Appendix~\ref{app:example-drawn}).

\textbf{The two policies learn different behaviors.} The Base's turns are mostly giving
information and persuasion, followed by simple reflections and open questions, with no other code
above $0.05$ of turns (training oracle; every code under both judges: Table~\ref{tab:codes}).
% \dnotedone{renamed: `turn-level reward' is now K=0}{k=0?}
% \dnotedone{reworded: `the distribution of therapist behaviours shifts'}{[the mix -?]}
% \dnotedone{renamed: `significantly', defined once in §3.3}{[clears Holm ?]}
Under $\Kz$ the distribution of therapist behaviors shifts, late and unevenly, toward
\textbf{non-specific praise}: from $0.04$ of the Base's turns to $0.22$ of the $\Kz$ policy's at
iteration 8, $0.08$ at iteration 9 and $0.41$ at iteration 10 (Figure~\ref{fig:process}a),
significantly more than under $\Kf$ at iterations 8 and 10. The dip at iteration 9 follows the one
late round of training, on candidates from the iteration-8 model, in which the $\Kz$ reward barely
favored praise (Appendix~\ref{app:premium}).
% \dnotedone{reworded: now `Under K=5 it' (the behaviour distribution)}{[the mix \textasciitilde{}]}
Under $\Kf$ it moves instead toward \textbf{complex reflections}: from $0.02$ of the Base's turns to
$0.23$ at iteration 10, significantly more than under $\Kz$ at every iteration from 4 on, and the
share of MI-consistent turns at iteration 10 is $0.44$ against $\Kz$'s $0.29$ (Base $0.31$). Both
policies lose open questions (from $0.09$ of the Base's turns to $0.00$ and $0.01$), and both
roughly triple the length of their turns, from $273$ characters at the Base to $896$ under $\Kz$
and $849$ under $\Kf$, near the 200-token cap.

The $\Kf$ policy also persuades more: persuasion, arguing for change on the patient's behalf (the
\emph{righting reflex} that MI warns against; \citealp{miller2013mi}), rises from $0.20$ of the
Base's turns to $0.28$ of its own (Figure~\ref{fig:process}c).
% \dnotedone{rephrased: says what Chiu et al. report}{[the failure mode \citet{chiu2024bolt} report for LLM therapists -?]}
It is the tendency \citet{chiu2024bolt} report in LLM therapists, which advise where an MI
counselor would be more likely to reflect. The share of MI-inconsistent turns still ends lower
under $\Kf$ ($0.33$ against $0.46$; Base $0.25$; Table~\ref{tab:process}), because of the $\Kz$
policy's late praise; the $\Kf$ policy's own share rises with its persuasion. The held-out judge
agrees on the direction of each change at iteration 10, but under it the $\Kf$ policy's praise
also rises, from the Base's $0.04$ to $0.20$ (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}).

\textbf{Corroboration without a judge.} Every code above comes from an LLM, so the praise finding
could in principle be a judge artifact. A \textbf{keyword marker} of ten fixed praise phrases
(``I'm so proud'', ``you got this'', \ldots; Appendix~\ref{app:marker}) involves no model. It
matches almost none of the Base's turns and stays low under $\Kf$; under $\Kz$ it jumps at
iteration 8, falls back at 9 and peaks at 10, the course of the coder's praise share (Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:overpraise}); its level is not comparable with the coder's, since it flags any turn
that contains a phrase. MICI agrees: $8.3$ of the $9.9$ MI-inconsistent acts per session it counts
for the $\Kz$ policy at iteration 10 ($84\%$) fall under its over-praise code, which we added to the
MISC's codes (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

The praise finding thus rests on three measures, two from the same judge and one with no model.

Sentence embeddings, which involve no LLM judge, show the $\Kz$ policy's replies varying less with
the patient from iteration 3 on: the share of their embedding variance that lies between
conversations falls from $0.39$ at the Base to $0.19$ at iteration 10, against $0.30$ under $\Kf$
(Appendix~\ref{app:embedding}). In the same kind of space, the direction in which each round of
$\Kz$ training moved the policy points toward praise sentences at training iterations 4--8 and 10
in each of five encoders, which the $\Kf$ direction does at no iteration in more than three
(Appendix~\ref{app:direction}).
