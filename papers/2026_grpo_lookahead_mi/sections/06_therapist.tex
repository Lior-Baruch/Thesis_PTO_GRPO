\section{What look-ahead teaches the therapist}
\label{sec:therapist}

A higher score is not by itself evidence of better therapy, so this section and the next ask what
the final policies do differently.

In the clearest case, the first reply to a reluctant 61-year-old smoker sent to therapy, the $\Kz$
policy answers ``Great to hear that you're ready to try again. That's very courageous [\ldots] I'm
so proud of you for taking this step'', praising a step she has not taken, and the $\Kf$ policy
``You're right, it's just a habit. It's been a lifelong struggle for you'', reflecting her failed
attempts before turning to advice and a question; both judges' coders label them non-specific
praise and complex reflection. The pair tops a ranking of such pairs, so it is an illustration
(Appendix~\ref{app:example-clear}; one drawn at random: Appendix~\ref{app:example-drawn}).

\textbf{The two policies learn different behaviors.} The Base's turns are mostly giving
information and persuasion, followed by simple reflections and open questions, with no other code
above $0.05$ of turns (Table~\ref{tab:codes}).
% \dnotedone{renamed: `turn-level reward' is now K=0}{k=0?}
% \dnotedone{reworded: `the distribution of therapist behaviours shifts'}{[the mix -?]}
% \dnotedone{renamed: `significantly', defined once in §3.3}{[clears Holm ?]}
Under $\Kz$ the distribution of therapist behaviors shifts, late and unevenly, toward
\textbf{non-specific praise}: from $0.04$ of the Base's turns to $0.22$ of the $\Kz$ policy's at
iteration 8, $0.08$ at iteration 9 and $0.41$ at iteration 10 (Figure~\ref{fig:process}a),
significantly more than under $\Kf$ at iterations 8 and 10. The dip at iteration 9 follows the one
late round of training, on candidates from the iteration-8 model, in which the $\Kz$ reward barely
favored praise (Appendix~\ref{app:premium}).
% \dnotedone{reworded: now `Under K=5 it' (the behaviour distribution)}{[the mix \textasciitilde{}]}
Under $\Kf$ it moves instead toward \textbf{complex reflections}: from $0.02$ of the Base's turns to
$0.23$ at iteration 10, significantly more than under $\Kz$ at every iteration from 4 on, and the
share of MI-consistent turns at iteration 10 is $0.44$ against $\Kz$'s $0.29$ (Base $0.31$). Both
policies lose open questions ($0.09$ of the Base's turns, $0.00$ and $0.01$) and roughly triple
their turn length ($273$ characters at the Base, $896$ and $849$), near the 200-token cap.

The $\Kf$ policy also persuades more: persuasion, arguing for change on the patient's behalf (the
\emph{righting reflex} that MI warns against; \citealp{miller2013mi}), rises from $0.20$ of the
Base's turns to $0.28$ of its own (Figure~\ref{fig:process}c).
% \dnotedone{rephrased: says what Chiu et al. report}{[the failure mode \citet{chiu2024bolt} report for LLM therapists -?]}
It is the tendency \citet{chiu2024bolt} report in LLM therapists, which advise where an MI
counselor would reflect. The MI-inconsistent share still ends lower under $\Kf$ ($0.33$ against
$0.46$; Base $0.25$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:process}) because of the $\Kz$ policy's late praise; the $\Kf$
policy's own share rises with its persuasion. The held-out judge agrees on the direction of each
change at iteration 10, but under it the $\Kf$ policy's praise also rises, from $0.04$ to $0.20$
(Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}).

\textbf{Corroboration without a judge.} The praise finding could in principle be a judge
artifact, but a \textbf{keyword marker} of ten fixed praise phrases (``I'm so proud'', ``you got
this'', \ldots; Appendix~\ref{app:marker}), which involves no model, follows the coder's praise
share: almost none of the Base's turns, low under $\Kf$, and under $\Kz$ a jump at iteration 8, a
fall at 9 and a peak at 10 (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:overpraise}; its level is not comparable). MICI agrees: $8.3$ of the $9.9$ MI-inconsistent acts per session it counts
for the $\Kz$ policy at iteration 10 ($84\%$) fall under its over-praise code, which we added to the
MISC's codes (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

The praise finding thus rests on three measures, two from the same judge and one with no model.

Sentence embeddings, which involve no LLM judge, show the $\Kz$ policy's replies varying less with
the patient from iteration 3 on: the share of their embedding variance that lies between
conversations falls from $0.39$ at the Base to $0.19$ at iteration 10, against $0.30$ under $\Kf$
(Appendix~\ref{app:embedding}). In the same space, each round of $\Kz$ training moved the policy
toward praise sentences at training iterations 4--8 and 10 in all five encoders tried, the $\Kf$
run at no iteration in more than three (Appendix~\ref{app:direction}).
