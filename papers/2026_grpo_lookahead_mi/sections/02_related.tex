\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Group-relative policy optimisation.}
GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} replaces PPO's learned value function
\citep{schulman2017ppo, ouyang2022instructgpt} with a baseline computed across a group of sampled
completions for the same prompt. That makes it attractive for dialogue, where a value function
would have to be learned over conversation states, but it also concentrates all the learning signal
in the \emph{relative} ranking of siblings --- so the quality of the reward that produces the
ranking is the whole ballgame. Our contribution is orthogonal to the loss: we change what the reward
function observes and leave the group-relative machinery untouched.

\paragraph{Look-ahead and search in preference learning.}
Using simulated continuations to score a candidate action is the standard move in planning, and it
has been imported into preference learning in several forms: MCTS-guided prompt and response search
\citep{yu2023promptmcts, xie2024mctsdpo}, process supervision that rewards intermediate steps by
their eventual outcome \citep{lightman2024letsverify}, and sampling-based reranking against a
reward model \citep{pace2024westofn}. The immediate predecessor of this work is preference tree
optimisation \citep{baruch2025pto}, which introduced the $K$-turn look-ahead lever for MI dialogue
and applied it to a preference-tree method with DPO \citep{rafailov2023dpo}. What has not been
tested, and is what we test, is whether that lever transfers to a group-relative optimizer, and
what it costs when the continuation must be simulated by an API-served patient.

\paragraph{Reward hacking and LLM judges.}
That optimising a proxy degrades the true objective is a long-standing concern
\citep{amodei2016concrete, skalse2022defining, pan2022effects} with a well-documented scaling
behaviour \citep{gao2023scaling}. LLM-as-judge evaluation \citep{zheng2023judging} adds specific
pathologies: sycophancy \citep{sharma2024sycophancy, perez2023discovering}, self-preference
\citep{panickssery2024selfpreference}, and length bias \citep{singhal2024long,
dubois2024lengthcontrolled}. Our over-praise result is a sycophancy finding with an unusual handle
on it --- the exploit is not merely present or absent across arms, it is \emph{selected} by the
horizon of the reward, and we can corroborate it with a deterministic transcript statistic rather
than a second model judge. The one-sided saturation we report in §\ref{sec:measurement} is, to our
knowledge, not a documented failure mode of judge-based evaluation, and it is invisible to the
arm-level agreement statistics usually reported.

\paragraph{Motivational interviewing and counselling dialogue systems.}
MI \citep{miller1991mi} has established behavioural coding schemes \citep{moyers2016miti} and
validated session instruments \citep{hatcher2006waisr, larsen1979csq}, which is what makes it a
usable testbed: the reward can be an instrument with published psychometrics rather than an ad hoc
rubric. Prior NLP work has assessed counselling quality automatically
\citep{perezrosas2019goodcounselor, yosef2024assessing} and used LLMs to scaffold or simulate
counselling interactions \citep{steenstra2025scaffolding, chen2025broaden}. We use MI not to build
a deployable counsellor --- see the Ethics Statement --- but because it is a domain where the
mismatch between locally-visible and downstream value is unusually explicit in the clinical
literature itself.
