\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Group-relative policy optimisation.} GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} replaces the learned
value function of PPO \citep{schulman2017ppo, ouyang2022instructgpt} with a baseline computed
across a group of sampled completions for the same prompt, and has since become the standard
optimiser for RL on language models \citep{guo2025deepseekr1}. For dialogue the trade is
attractive, since a value function would have to be learned over conversation states, but it
concentrates the entire learning signal in the \emph{relative} ranking of siblings, so the reward
that produces the ranking is everything. We change what the reward observes and nothing else.

\textbf{Multi-turn RL for dialogue.} That a dialogue turn should be credited for its downstream
consequences is the founding premise of RL for dialogue \citep{li2016deeprl}, pursued since by
learning utterance-level value functions \citep{zhou2024archer}, by training on imagined
conversations with a simulated interlocutor \citep{hong2023imagined}, by self-play against
LLM-rated social goals \citep{wang2024sotopiapi}, and by preference feedback over whole
conversations \citep{shani2024multiturn}. GRPO itself has been carried into multi-turn settings by
turn-level credit assignment in tool-use agents \citep{wei2025multiturn} and by rollouts against
LLM-simulated users \citep{qian2025userrl}; both keep the reward the environment returns and
change how it is distributed over turns. Look-ahead instead changes what the reward is computed
on: it learns no value function and changes no loss, but estimates a turn's downstream value by
Monte Carlo, rolling the conversation forward under the current policy and scoring the result.

\textbf{Look-ahead and search in preference learning.} Scoring a candidate by a simulated
continuation is the standard move in planning, imported into preference learning as MCTS-guided
search \citep{yu2023promptmcts, xie2024mctsdpo}, process supervision \citep{lightman2024letsverify},
and reranking against a reward model \citep{pace2024westofn}. The direct predecessor of this work is
PTO \citep{baruch2025pto}, which introduced $K$-turn look-ahead for MI dialogue: candidate turns are
branched at each patient turn, each is scored by an oracle shown its simulated continuation, and the
best and worst become a DPO pair \citep{rafailov2023dpo}. We keep the lever and change everything
downstream of it: no tree, no pair selection, every scored candidate trains.

\textbf{Reward hacking and LLM judges.} That optimising a proxy degrades the true objective is a
long-standing concern \citep{amodei2016concrete, skalse2022defining, pan2022effects, gao2023scaling},
and LLM-as-judge evaluation \citep{zheng2023judging} adds sycophancy \citep{sharma2024sycophancy,
perez2023discovering}, self-preference \citep{panickssery2024selfpreference}, and length bias
\citep{singhal2024long, dubois2024lengthcontrolled}. Our over-praise result is a sycophancy finding
with an unusual handle: the exploit is \emph{selected} by the horizon of the reward, and it is
corroborated by a deterministic transcript statistic rather than a second model judge. The
one-sided grader saturation of \S\ref{sec:measurement} is the variance side of reward-model
over-optimisation \citep{gao2023scaling}: a trained-against grader losing discrimination over
conversations that an independent grader still tells apart, which to our knowledge has not been
isolated for LLM judges. Reward-model ensembles mitigate over-optimisation in training
\citep{coste2024ensembles}; our second grader plays the analogous role in evaluation.

\textbf{Motivational interviewing.} MI \citep{miller1991mi} has established coding schemes
\citep{moyers2016miti}, validated session instruments \citep{hatcher2006waisr, larsen1979csq}, and
an expert-annotated corpus of high- and low-quality sessions \citep{wu2022annomi}, so the reward
can be an instrument with published psychometrics rather than an ad hoc rubric. Prior work assesses
counselling quality automatically \citep{perezrosas2019goodcounselor, yosef2024assessing},
simulates counselling with LLMs \citep{steenstra2025scaffolding, chen2025broaden}, and codes LLM
therapists, which resemble low-quality therapy by advising where a counsellor would reflect
\citep{chiu2024bolt}, the directive residue we find under look-ahead (\S\ref{sec:behaviour}). We
use MI not to build a deployable counsellor (Ethics Statement) but because the gap between locally
visible and downstream value is unusually explicit in its literature.
