\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Group-relative policy optimisation.} GRPO \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} replaces the learned
value function of PPO \citep{schulman2017ppo, ouyang2022instructgpt} with a baseline computed
across a group of sampled completions for the same prompt, and has since become the standard
optimiser for RL on language models \citep{guo2025deepseekr1}. For dialogue the trade is
attractive, since a value function would have to be learned over conversation states, but it
concentrates the entire learning signal in the \emph{relative} ranking of siblings, so the reward
that produces the ranking is everything. We change what the reward observes and leave the
group-relative machinery untouched.

\textbf{Multi-turn RL for dialogue.} That a dialogue turn should be credited for its downstream
consequences is the founding premise of RL for dialogue \citep{li2016deeprl}, and recent work
pursues it by learning utterance-level value functions \citep{zhou2024archer}, by training on
imagined conversations with a simulated interlocutor \citep{hong2023imagined}, by self-play against
LLM-rated social goals \citep{wang2024sotopiapi}, and by preference feedback over whole
conversations \citep{shani2024multiturn}. Look-ahead is the simplest member of this family: it
learns no value function and changes no loss, but estimates a turn's downstream value by Monte
Carlo, rolling the conversation forward under the current policy and scoring the result.

\textbf{Look-ahead and search in preference learning.} Scoring a candidate by a simulated
continuation is the standard move in planning, imported into preference learning as MCTS-guided
search \citep{yu2023promptmcts, xie2024mctsdpo}, process supervision \citep{lightman2024letsverify},
and reranking against a reward model \citep{pace2024westofn}. The direct predecessor of this work is
PTO \citep{baruch2025pto}, which introduced $K$-turn look-ahead for MI dialogue: candidate turns are
branched at each patient turn, each is scored by an oracle shown its simulated continuation, and the
best and worst become a DPO pair \citep{rafailov2023dpo}. We keep the lever and change everything
downstream of it: there is no tree and no pair selection, every scored candidate trains through
the group-relative advantage, and the horizon reweights every gradient rather than choosing which
pair survives.

\textbf{Reward hacking and LLM judges.} That optimising a proxy degrades the true objective is a
long-standing concern \citep{amodei2016concrete, skalse2022defining, pan2022effects, gao2023scaling},
and LLM-as-judge evaluation \citep{zheng2023judging} adds sycophancy \citep{sharma2024sycophancy,
perez2023discovering}, self-preference \citep{panickssery2024selfpreference}, and length bias
\citep{singhal2024long, dubois2024lengthcontrolled}. Our over-praise result is a sycophancy finding
with an unusual handle: the exploit is \emph{selected} by the horizon of the reward, and it is
corroborated by a deterministic transcript statistic rather than a second model judge. The
one-sided grader saturation of \S\ref{sec:measurement} is, to our knowledge, not a documented
failure mode of judge-based evaluation.

\textbf{Motivational interviewing.} MI \citep{miller1991mi} has established coding schemes
\citep{moyers2016miti} and validated session instruments \citep{hatcher2006waisr, larsen1979csq},
so the reward can be an instrument with published psychometrics rather than an ad hoc rubric.
Prior work assesses counselling quality automatically \citep{perezrosas2019goodcounselor,
yosef2024assessing} and simulates counselling with LLMs \citep{steenstra2025scaffolding,
chen2025broaden}. We use MI not to build a deployable counsellor (Ethics Statement) but because
the gap between locally visible and downstream value is unusually explicit in its literature.
