\textbf{The final policies.} At iteration 10 the $\Kf$ policy leads on every instrument, under
both judges, against the last and against the best checkpoint of the $\Kz$ run (iteration 8, its
highest \QQ\ under the training oracle; Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}). On the reward rubric, scored on a
1--5 scale, the gap is $+0.76$ ($\dz{=}0.91$) against the last checkpoint and $+0.43$
($\dz{=}0.74$) against the best.
% \dnotedone{cut}{[, and MI-inconsistent behaviour shows the largest standardised effect of all - ??]}
Against the Base, both runs improve significantly on every instrument except MICI, which neither
improves: the $\Kf$ policy ends level with the Base ($0.21$ MI-inconsistent acts per therapist turn
against $0.21$) and the $\Kz$ policy above it ($0.84$).

\textbf{Onset, and the $\Kz$ decline.} For the first three iterations the two runs do not differ
on \QQ\ (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}); $\Kf$ leads from iteration 4, the other instruments follow the
same course (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}, last column), and the one significant difference in $\Kz$'s
favor is MICI at iteration 3. It is not only $\Kf$ climbing: $\Kz$ peaks at iteration 8 and falls
over its last two iterations, which is why Table~\ref{tab:endpoint} sets the final $\Kf$ policy
against both checkpoints. Averaged over all ten iterations, so that no endpoint is chosen, every
instrument favors $\Kf$ under both judges, though over iterations 1--7 alone MICI favors $\Kz$ under
the training oracle (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). The held-out judge agrees
(Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}): $\Kf$ leads significantly on \QQ\ at every iteration from 4 and, against
$\Kz$'s iteration-8 checkpoint, on seven of the nine rows, all but Q2 and WAI-SR; on MICI both
policies end above its Base ($0.63$ and $1.05$ against $0.36$). A second draw of 96 conversations
from the final $\Kf$ policy changes no row significantly ($|\dz| < 0.18$ under both judges;
Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).
