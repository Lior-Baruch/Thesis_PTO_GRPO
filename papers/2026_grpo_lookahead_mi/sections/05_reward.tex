\textbf{Endpoint.} At the matched iteration-10 endpoint, look-ahead leads on every instrument
under both graders (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}). On the rewarded rubric the gap is $+0.765$
($\dz\,0.905$) under the training oracle and $+0.616$ ($\dz\,1.030$) under the held-out judge;
that the held-out judge reports the \emph{larger} standardised effect is consistent with a gain
that is not specific to the optimisation target. The instruments outside the reward move the same
way, every row Holm-significant at $p<.001$, so the improvement is not confined to what was
optimised. MI-inconsistent behaviour shows the largest standardised effect of all;
\S\ref{sec:behaviour} takes it apart.

\textbf{Gain relative to the turn-level arm.} Against each arm's own base (indistinguishable:
$2.963$ vs.\ $3.067$, $\dz\,0.115$, n.s.), the endpoint gains on \QQ\ are $+1.554$ vs.\ $+0.686$
under the training oracle and $+1.038$ vs.\ $+0.396$ under the held-out judge, ratios of
$2.27\times$ and $2.62\times$. Those ratios are anchored on the $\Kz$ arm's \emph{last}
checkpoint, which is not its best; against its best checkpoint under each grader they are
$1.53\times$ ($1.554/1.016$, iteration 8) and $1.34\times$ ($1.038/0.776$, iteration 3). The gain
is thus $1.3$ to $2.6$ times the turn-level gain, depending on the $\Kz$ arm's trajectory.

\textbf{Onset, and the $\Kz$ decline.} For the first three iterations the two arms are
indistinguishable on \QQ\ under both graders (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). The advantage opens at
iteration 4, is Holm-significant at 6 of 10 iterations under each grader (training oracle: 4,
6--10; held-out: 4--7, 9, 10), and is at its widest over iterations 9--10. It is not only $\Kf$
climbing: in this run the $\Kz$ arm peaks at iteration 8 ($4.082$) and falls to $3.753$ over its
last two iterations, while under the held-out judge its best state is iteration~3
(see \S\ref{sec:behaviour}). Early stopping does not rescue it: against the $\Kz$
arm's \emph{best} checkpoint under either grader's selection, the $\Kf$ endpoint still leads on
\QQ\ under both graders (vs.\ iteration 8: $+0.435$ / $+0.256$, $\dz\,0.743$ / $0.384$; vs.\
iteration 3: $+0.524$ / $+0.236$, $\dz\,0.977$ / $0.386$; all Holm-significant).

\textbf{Replication of the endpoint.} Every state here is scored on one 96-conversation sample,
so we re-drew the winning one: 96 fresh conversations from the same $\Kf$ iteration-10 adapter,
personas, and decoding, scored by both graders. Across 9 instruments and both graders the two
draws of the same policy differ by at most $|\dz|\,0.174$, nothing significant. Substituting the
replicate, the $\Kf - \Kz$ gap on \QQ\ is $+0.709$ ($\dz\,0.919$) / $+0.637$ ($\dz\,0.949$)
against $+0.765$ / $+0.616$ originally: same sign and significance, effect sizes within $0.09$,
endpoint level $4.517 \rightarrow 4.461$. The re-draw bounds evaluation noise at the contested
endpoint, not training variance (Limitations).
