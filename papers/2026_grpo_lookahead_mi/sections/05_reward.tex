\section{Look-ahead more than doubles the gain}
\label{sec:reward}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\textwidth]{figures/k_headline_q1q2_grpo.png}
\caption{\QQ\ by iteration under each grader: mean $\pm$ SE over the 96 personas, each arm's own
base as a dotted reference, endpoint means printed at iteration 10. Iteration 0 is each arm's
independent base draw. A star marks an iteration at which the persona-paired $\Kf$ vs.\ $\Kz$
contrast clears Holm (per-iteration deltas and effect sizes in Table~\ref{tab:byiter}). The two
panels' $y$-axes are independent; grader levels are never comparable.}
\label{fig:headline}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{The headline.} At the matched iteration-10 endpoint, look-ahead wins on every instrument
under both graders (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}). On the rewarded rubric the gap is $+0.765$
($\dz\,0.905$) under the training oracle and $+0.616$ ($\dz\,1.030$) under the held-out judge.
The held-out judge reports the \emph{larger} standardised effect, so the gain is not an artifact
of the optimisation target. Against each arm's own base, look-ahead more than
doubles what the same optimizer extracts from the same oracle: $+1.554$ vs.\ $+0.686$ under the
training oracle ($2.27\times$) and $+1.038$ vs.\ $+0.396$ under the held-out judge ($2.62\times$),
from bases that are statistically indistinguishable ($2.963$ vs.\ $3.067$; $\dz\,0.115$, n.s.).
The $\Kf$ policy ends at $4.517$ on a 1--5 scale against $3.753$ for $\Kz$.

The instruments outside the reward move too, which is what separates a genuine gain from a
target-specific one. MI-SAT ($\dz\,0.531$ / $0.829$ under the two graders), CSQ-8 ($0.482$ /
$0.678$), WAI-SR ($0.513$ / $0.442$), the MITI coder ($0.735$ / $0.502$), and patient change talk
($0.516$ / $0.563$) all favour look-ahead under both graders, every row Holm-significant at
$p<.001$. MI-inconsistent behaviour, the one lower-is-better axis, shows the largest standardised
effect of all ($\dz\,-1.862$ / $-1.567$); \S\ref{sec:behaviour} takes it apart, because it is where
the two graders stop agreeing about magnitude.

\textbf{The effect has an onset, and $\Kz$ does not hold what it gains.} For the first three
iterations the two arms are indistinguishable on \QQ\ under both graders
(Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). The advantage opens at iteration 4, is Holm-significant at 6 of 10
iterations under each grader (training oracle: 4, 6--10; held-out: 4--7, 9, 10), and is largest at
the endpoint. It is not a single-iteration artifact: the gap clears Holm at every iteration from 6
to 10 under the training oracle. And it is not only $\Kf$ climbing: the $\Kz$ arm peaks at
iteration 8 ($4.082$) and falls to $3.753$ over its last two iterations, while under the held-out
judge its best state is iteration~3. \S\ref{sec:behaviour} identifies what that policy was doing
over exactly those iterations. Early stopping does not rescue it: against the $\Kz$ arm's
\emph{best} checkpoint under either grader's selection, the $\Kf$ endpoint still leads on \QQ\
under both graders ($+0.435$ / $+0.256$, $\dz\,0.743$ / $0.384$ against iteration 8; $+0.524$ /
$+0.236$, $\dz\,0.977$ / $0.386$ against iteration 3; all Holm-significant).

\textbf{The endpoint survives a second draw.} Every state here is scored on one 96-conversation
sample, so we re-drew the winning one: 96 fresh conversations from the same $\Kf$ iteration-10
adapter, same personas, same unseeded decoding, scored on all eight instruments by both graders.
This gives a noise floor at a \emph{trained} checkpoint: across 9 instruments and both graders,
the two draws of the same policy differ by at most $|\dz|\,0.174$, nothing significant. Substituting the replicate, the $\Kf - \Kz$ gap on \QQ\ is $+0.709$
($\dz\,0.919$) / $+0.637$ ($\dz\,0.949$) against $+0.765$ / $+0.616$ originally: same sign, same
significance, effect sizes within $0.08$. The endpoint level moves $4.517 \rightarrow 4.461$, the
honest precision of a single-draw score. The re-draw covers the $\Kf$ side only, so it tests the
contested endpoint rather than the whole contrast.
