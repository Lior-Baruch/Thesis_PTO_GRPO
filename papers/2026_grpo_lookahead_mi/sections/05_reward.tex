\textbf{The headline.} At the matched iteration-10 endpoint, look-ahead wins on every instrument
under both graders (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}). On the rewarded rubric the gap is $+0.765$
($\dz\,0.905$) under the training oracle and $+0.616$ ($\dz\,1.030$) under the held-out judge, and
the held-out judge reports the \emph{larger} standardised effect, so the gain is not an artifact of
the optimisation target. The instruments outside the reward move the same way, every row
Holm-significant at $p<.001$, which separates a genuine gain from a target-specific one.
MI-inconsistent behaviour shows the largest standardised effect of all; \S\ref{sec:behaviour}
takes it apart.

\textbf{How much more the same oracle yields.} Against each arm's own base (indistinguishable:
$2.963$ vs.\ $3.067$, $\dz\,0.115$, n.s.), the endpoint gains on \QQ\ are $+1.554$ vs.\ $+0.686$
under the training oracle and $+1.038$ vs.\ $+0.396$ under the held-out judge, ratios of
$2.27\times$ and $2.62\times$. Those ratios are anchored on the $\Kz$ arm's \emph{last}
checkpoint, which is not its best. Read against its best checkpoint under each grader, they are
$1.53\times$ ($1.554/1.016$, iteration 8) and $1.34\times$ ($1.038/0.776$, iteration 3). The gain
is thus $1.3$ to $2.6$ times the turn-level gain; where in that range depends on the $\Kz$ arm's
trajectory, described next.

\textbf{The effect has an onset, and $\Kz$ does not hold what it gains.} For the first three
iterations the two arms are indistinguishable on \QQ\ under both graders
(Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). The advantage opens at iteration 4, is Holm-significant at 6 of 10
iterations under each grader (training oracle: 4, 6--10; held-out: 4--7, 9, 10), and is largest at
the endpoint. It is not only $\Kf$ climbing: the $\Kz$ arm peaks at iteration 8 ($4.082$) and
falls to $3.753$ over its last two iterations, while under the held-out judge its best state is
iteration~3 (\S\ref{sec:behaviour} says why). Early stopping does not rescue it: against the $\Kz$ arm's \emph{best} checkpoint
under either grader's selection, the $\Kf$ endpoint still leads on \QQ\ under both graders
(vs.\ iteration 8: $+0.435$ / $+0.256$, $\dz\,0.743$ / $0.384$; vs.\ iteration 3: $+0.524$ /
$+0.236$, $\dz\,0.977$ / $0.386$; all Holm-significant).

\textbf{The endpoint survives a second draw.} Every state here is scored on one 96-conversation
sample, so we re-drew the winning one: 96 fresh conversations from the same $\Kf$ iteration-10
adapter, personas, and decoding, scored by both graders. Across 9 instruments and both graders the
two draws of the same policy differ by at most $|\dz|\,0.174$, nothing significant, a noise floor
at a \emph{trained} checkpoint. Substituting the replicate, the $\Kf - \Kz$ gap on \QQ\ is
$+0.709$ ($\dz\,0.919$) / $+0.637$ ($\dz\,0.949$) against $+0.765$ / $+0.616$ originally: same
sign and significance, effect sizes within $0.09$, endpoint level $4.517 \rightarrow 4.461$. The
re-draw covers the $\Kf$ side only, so it tests the contested endpoint, not the whole contrast.
