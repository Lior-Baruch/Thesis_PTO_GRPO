\textbf{The headline.} At the matched iteration-10 endpoint, look-ahead wins on every instrument
under both graders (Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}). On the rewarded rubric the gap is $+0.765$
($\dz\,0.905$) under the training oracle and $+0.616$ ($\dz\,1.030$) under the held-out judge.
The held-out judge reports the \emph{larger} standardised effect, so the gain is not an artifact
of the optimisation target. Against each arm's own base, look-ahead more than doubles what the same
optimiser extracts from the same oracle: $+1.554$ vs.\ $+0.686$ under the training oracle
($2.27\times$) and $+1.038$ vs.\ $+0.396$ under the held-out judge ($2.62\times$), from bases that
are statistically indistinguishable ($2.963$ vs.\ $3.067$; $\dz\,0.115$, n.s.).

The instruments outside the reward move too, which separates a genuine gain from a
target-specific one: MI-SAT ($\dz\,0.531$ / $0.829$ under the two graders), CSQ-8 ($0.482$ /
$0.678$), WAI-SR ($0.513$ / $0.442$), the MITI coder ($0.735$ / $0.502$), and patient change talk
($0.516$ / $0.563$) all favour look-ahead under both graders, every row Holm-significant at
$p<.001$. MI-inconsistent behaviour, the one lower-is-better axis, shows the largest standardised
effect of all ($\dz\,-1.862$ / $-1.567$); \S\ref{sec:behaviour} takes it apart, because it is where
the two graders stop agreeing about magnitude.

\textbf{The effect has an onset, and $\Kz$ does not hold what it gains.} For the first three
iterations the two arms are indistinguishable on \QQ\ under both graders
(Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). The advantage opens at iteration 4, is Holm-significant at 6 of 10
iterations under each grader (training oracle: 4, 6--10; held-out: 4--7, 9, 10), and is largest at
the endpoint. It is not only $\Kf$ climbing: the $\Kz$ arm peaks at iteration 8 ($4.082$) and
falls to $3.753$ over its last two iterations, while under the held-out judge its best state is
iteration~3; \S\ref{sec:behaviour} identifies what that policy was doing over exactly those
iterations. Early stopping does not rescue it: against the $\Kz$ arm's \emph{best} checkpoint
under either grader's selection, the $\Kf$ endpoint still leads on \QQ\ under both graders
(vs.\ iteration 8: $+0.435$ / $+0.256$, $\dz\,0.743$ / $0.384$; vs.\ iteration 3: $+0.524$ /
$+0.236$, $\dz\,0.977$ / $0.386$; all Holm-significant).

\textbf{The endpoint survives a second draw.} Every state here is scored on one 96-conversation
sample, so we re-drew the winning one: 96 fresh conversations from the same $\Kf$ iteration-10
adapter, personas, and decoding, scored on all eight instruments by both graders. This gives a
noise floor at a \emph{trained} checkpoint: across 9 instruments and both graders, the two draws
of the same policy differ by at most $|\dz|\,0.174$, nothing significant.
Substituting the replicate, the $\Kf - \Kz$ gap on \QQ\ is $+0.709$ ($\dz\,0.919$) / $+0.637$
($\dz\,0.949$) against $+0.765$ / $+0.616$ originally: same sign, same significance, effect sizes
within $0.09$; the endpoint level moves $4.517 \rightarrow 4.461$. The re-draw covers the $\Kf$
side only, so it tests the contested endpoint rather than the whole contrast. (An audit of what
the $\Kf$ reward observed inside its rollout window is in the Limitations.)
