\section*{Ethics Statement}

\textbf{No human subjects, and no clinical claim.} Every conversation in this work is between two
language models. No human patient data was collected, used, or generated, and no human participants
were involved at any stage. Nothing here is evidence that the resulting model is safe or effective
for use with people. A 1B-parameter model trained against a simulated patient and graded by another
language model is a research artifact; motivational interviewing is a clinical skill practised with
people in distress, and the gap between the two is not bridged by any number in this paper.

\textbf{The failure mode we document is a safety-relevant one.} The $K{=}0$ policy learned to
praise its patient more or less regardless of what they said, and its score went \emph{up} while it
did so. In a counselling setting that behaviour is not a neutral artifact of optimisation: unearned
affirmation of ambivalence or of a harmful plan is precisely the error motivational interviewing
trains counsellors out of. We report it prominently, including the residual drift that survives
under look-ahead (\S\ref{sec:behaviour-honest}), because a reader evaluating this line of work
should see the failure mode as clearly as the improvement. We would consider it a misuse of this
paper to cite the headline gain without the behavioural result.

\textbf{Simulated patients encode a narrow population.} The 96 personas vary six attributes around
two presenting problems (smoking and obesity). They are a coarse and culturally narrow caricature
of help-seeking, they were authored by the researchers rather than derived from clinical data, and
results on them should not be read as generalising across presenting problems, demographics, or
cultures. The saturated design (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}) means we cannot even measure
generalisation within this grid.

\textbf{Judges inherit their models' biases.} Both graders are commercial LLMs whose training data
and alignment procedures are undisclosed. They disagree systematically about level (1.2--1.7
points) and, as \S\ref{sec:measurement} shows, can disagree severely about individual
conversations. Using such models as the sole arbiter of therapeutic quality is a methodological
convenience, not a validated measurement, and any downstream use of this reward signal should treat
it as such.

\textbf{Compute and models.} Training used a single cloud A100-class GPU for approximately
$27.9 + 51.2 = 79.1$ GPU-hours across the two arms reported here, plus API inference for the
patient simulator and the graders. The base model is openly licensed; the patient simulator and
graders are commercial APIs used within their terms of service.
