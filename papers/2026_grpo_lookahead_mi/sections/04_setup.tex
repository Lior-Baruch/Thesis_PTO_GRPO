\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task, simulator, oracle.} A session is a text conversation between a therapist policy and
a simulated patient who is ambivalent about a health behaviour. The patient is
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} conditioned on one of \textbf{96} persona system prompts, the exhaustive
enumeration of $2\ \text{gender} \times 3\ \text{cooperation} \times 2\ \text{problem} \times
2\ \text{duration} \times 2\ \text{prior attempts} \times 2\ \text{age}$
(Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). A session runs until the patient closes it or a cap of 49 utterances
after the scripted opening is reached; most close earlier, and base-policy sessions average 28--29
utterances. The therapist is \texttt{Llama-3.2-1B} under a fixed counsellor system prompt,
trained with LoRA adapters; responses are capped at 200 tokens, and Table~\ref{tab:config} lists
every hyperparameter and decoding setting. The oracle is the same \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} with
JSON-schema-constrained output. The \emph{training} reward is the mean of Q1 and Q2 below,
matching \citet{baruch2025pto}.

\textbf{Why MI.} MI's constructs are defined by the patient's response rather than by the
counsellor's utterance: its target outcome is \emph{change talk}, the patient's own statements in
favour of change, as against \emph{sustain talk}, and the MITI coding scheme rates a session on
\emph{cultivating change talk} and \emph{softening sustain talk}, properties of the trajectory
rather than of any one turn \citep{miller2013mi, moyers2016miti}. The value of a therapist turn is
therefore inherently delayed, which is what a reward horizon is meant to capture, and the field
supplies validated instruments from both the patient's and a trained coder's perspective.

\textbf{Instruments.} Evaluation uses eight instruments, each administered by a grader LLM shown
the full transcript (items and prompts in Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}). Q1 and Q2 are the
LLM-evaluator questionnaires of \citet{yosef2024assessing}, 5 and 17 patient-perspective Likert
items on session satisfaction and on alliance and relational communication, shown there to
separate levels of therapist expertise. WAI-SR \citep{hatcher2006waisr} and
CSQ-8 \citep{larsen1979csq} are published patient-report instruments, and MI-SAT is a six-item
intervention-satisfaction survey adapted for this task. Three coders count behaviour: a
MITI~4.2.1-style coder \citep{moyers2016miti} whose four global ratings are averaged; a patient
change-talk coder (PCT; the share of change talk among change and sustain talk, the patient-side
outcome MI aims at); and an MI-inconsistent-behaviour coder (MICI; coded acts per therapist turn,
lower is better). \QQ\ is thus both the optimisation target and a reported outcome
(\S\ref{sec:measurement}, Limitations), which is why a held-out grader is central here.

\textbf{Terminology.} The two scoring models are the \textbf{training oracle} (\primaryjudge,
which also plays the patient) and the \textbf{held-out judge} (\heldoutjudge, a different model
family that never touched training); \emph{grader} refers to either. The eight \emph{instruments}
are five patient-perspective Likert rubrics and three behaviour \emph{coders} (MITI, PCT, MICI).

\textbf{The two arms.} The runs are a controlled pair: their tracked run configurations differ in
exactly two fields, the look-ahead depth and its batching size (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
Everything else, from $G{=}8$, KL $\beta{=}0.01$ and the sampling temperature to the seed, the
oracle and the persona set, is identical (Table~\ref{tab:config}).

\textbf{Evaluation and statistics.} Every model state is scored on all eight instruments for all
96 personas by both graders. All contrasts are \textbf{persona-paired} (personas are reshuffled
every iteration, so pairing is on the recovered persona identity), with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests,
Cohen's \dz, bootstrap intervals, and Holm correction within each family of tests. Grader
\emph{levels} are not comparable (on \QQ\ the held-out judge is harsher by 1.1--1.8 points, varying
across the 22 states), so only contrasts cross graders and the two are never averaged.
