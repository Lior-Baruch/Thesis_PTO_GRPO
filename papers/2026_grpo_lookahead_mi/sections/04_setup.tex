\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task, simulator, oracle.} The therapist is \texttt{Llama-3.2-1B} (bf16, LoRA $r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}16$, lr $1\mathrm{e}{-}5$, seed 42). The patient is \texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}
conditioned on one of \textbf{96} persona system prompts, the exhaustive enumeration of
$2\ \text{gender} \times 3\ \text{cooperation} \times 2\ \text{problem} \times 2\ \text{duration}
\times 2\ \text{prior attempts} \times 2\ \text{age}$. A session runs to a target of 49 turns with
a 200-token cap per response and a 2{,}048-token therapist context; therapist decoding is at
temperature 0.9, the patient at 0.7 (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The oracle is the same
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} with JSON-schema-constrained output. The \emph{training} reward is the mean
of two validated MI questionnaires, Q1 (session satisfaction) and Q2 (working alliance), 22 Likert
items in total, matching \citet{baruch2025pto}.

\textbf{Instruments.} Evaluation uses eight instruments: Q1 and Q2, WAI-SR
\citep{hatcher2006waisr}, CSQ-8 \citep{larsen1979csq}, MI-SAT, a MITI-style behaviour coder
\citep{moyers2016miti}, patient change talk (PCT), and MI-inconsistent behaviour (MICI, a count of
coded MI-inconsistent acts; lower is better). \QQ\ is thus both the optimisation target and a
reported outcome; \S\ref{sec:measurement} and the Limitations treat that circularity explicitly,
and it is why a held-out grader is central rather than supplementary.

\textbf{The two arms.} The runs are a controlled pair, and this is checkable rather than asserted:
their tracked run configurations differ in exactly two substantive fields, the look-ahead depth
and its batching mirror (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). Everything else is identical: $G{=}8$, KL
$\beta{=}0.01$, sampling temperature 1.2, batch $64 \times 2$ accumulation, learning rate, seed,
MCL${=}12$, 96 conversations and 2 epochs per iteration, 10 iterations, oracle, and persona set.

\textbf{Evaluation and statistics.} Every model state is scored on all eight instruments for all
96 personas by two graders: the \textbf{training oracle} (\primaryjudge) and a \textbf{held-out
judge} (\heldoutjudge), a different model family that never played the patient and never touched
training. All contrasts are \textbf{persona-paired} (personas are reshuffled every iteration, so
pairing is on the recovered persona identity), with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Cohen's \dz,
2{,}000-resample percentile bootstrap intervals at a fixed seed, and Holm correction within each
family of tests. Two rules hold throughout. Grader \emph{levels} are not comparable (the held-out
judge is harsher by 1.2--1.7 points, model-dependently), so only contrasts cross graders and the
two are never averaged: an optimisation target and a held-out test are not two raters. And every
behavioural claim names its denominator, because the arms differ in both turn count and turn
length.
