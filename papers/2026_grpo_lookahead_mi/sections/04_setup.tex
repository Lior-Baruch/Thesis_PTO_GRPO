\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Task, simulator, oracle.} The therapist is \texttt{Llama-3.2-1B} (bf16, LoRA $r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}16$, lr $1\mathrm{e}{-}5$, seed 42) under a fixed counsellor system prompt. The
patient is \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} conditioned on one of \textbf{96}
persona system prompts, the exhaustive enumeration of
$2\ \text{gender} \times 3\ \text{cooperation} \times 2\ \text{problem} \times 2\ \text{duration}
\times 2\ \text{prior attempts} \times 2\ \text{age}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). A session runs
until the patient closes it or a cap of 49 utterances after the scripted opening is reached; most
close earlier, and base-policy sessions average 28--29 utterances. Responses are capped at 200 tokens within a
2{,}048-token therapist context; therapist decoding is at temperature 0.9, the patient at 0.7
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The oracle is the same \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} with JSON-schema-constrained
output. The \emph{training} reward is the mean of Q1 and Q2 below, matching \citet{baruch2025pto}.

\textbf{Instruments.} Evaluation uses eight instruments, each administered by a grader LLM shown
the full transcript (items and prompts in Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}). Q1 and Q2 are the
LLM-evaluator questionnaires of \citet{yosef2024assessing}, 5 and 17 patient-perspective Likert
items on session satisfaction and on alliance and relational communication, shown there to be
reliable and to separate levels of therapist expertise. WAI-SR \citep{hatcher2006waisr} and
CSQ-8 \citep{larsen1979csq} are published patient-report instruments, and MI-SAT is a six-item
intervention-satisfaction survey adapted for this task. Three coders count behaviour: a
MITI~4.2.1-style coder \citep{moyers2016miti} whose four global ratings are averaged, a patient
change-talk coder (PCT; the share of change talk among change and sustain talk), and an
MI-inconsistent-behaviour coder (MICI; coded acts per therapist turn, lower is better). \QQ\ is
thus both the optimisation target and a reported outcome; \S\ref{sec:measurement} and the
Limitations treat that circularity explicitly, and it is why a held-out grader is central here.

\textbf{The two arms.} The runs are a controlled pair, checkable rather than asserted: their
tracked run configurations differ in exactly two fields, the look-ahead depth and its batching
size (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). Everything else is identical (Table~\ref{tab:config}): $G{=}8$,
KL $\beta{=}0.01$, sampling temperature 1.2, learning rate, seed, MCL${=}12$, 96 conversations and
2 epochs per iteration, 10 iterations, oracle, and persona set.

\textbf{Evaluation and statistics.} Every model state is scored on all eight instruments for all
96 personas by two graders: the \textbf{training oracle} (\primaryjudge) and a \textbf{held-out
judge} (\heldoutjudge), a different model family that never touched training. All contrasts are \textbf{persona-paired} (personas are reshuffled every iteration, so
pairing is on the recovered persona identity), with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Cohen's \dz,
bootstrap intervals, and Holm correction within each family of tests. Grader \emph{levels} are not comparable (on \QQ\ the held-out judge is harsher by
1.1--1.8 points, varying across the 22 states), so only contrasts cross graders and the two are
never averaged.
