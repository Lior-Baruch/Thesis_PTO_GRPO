\begin{abstract}
When an LLM judge supplies the reward for a dialogue policy, it is usually shown the candidate
turn and nothing after it. In multi-turn goal-oriented dialogue that is a short-sighted proxy: the
turn that reads best on its own is often not the turn that leads anywhere good. We present
\textbf{\LAGRPO}: before the judge scores a candidate therapist turn, the patient simulator and the
current policy continue the conversation for $K$ further turns, and the judge scores the
continuation. The idea comes from preference-tree optimisation for motivational interviewing (MI);
we move it to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where the reward horizon reweights every
sampled candidate instead of selecting a preference pair. In a controlled pair of runs, identical
but for the horizon, $K{=}5$ more than doubles what GRPO extracts from the same judge ($+1.55$
vs.\ $+0.69$ rubric points over base under the training oracle, $+1.04$ vs.\ $+0.40$ under a
held-out judge from another model family), leads on all eight evaluation instruments under both
graders, and replicates on a fresh evaluation draw. It also changes what is learned. Turn-level
reward teaches flattery: a judge-free lexical marker finds over-praise in $67\%$ of the $K{=}0$
policy's therapist turns at the endpoint against $6\%$ under $K{=}5$, which asks questions
instead. Two caveats travel with the result. Under the held-out judge the look-ahead policy's
MI-inconsistency still rises, at under half the turn-level rate; and at the best checkpoint the
training oracle saturates one-sidedly, losing three-quarters of its per-conversation variance
while the held-out judge's does not move. The arm-level ordering survives both; the
per-conversation scores at that checkpoint do not.
\end{abstract}
