\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning has improved language models most on verifiable tasks, like mathematics. In
goal-oriented dialogue, an LLM judge usually supplies the reward, scoring each turn without seeing
what follows. Yet, as in chess, a move is worth what it leads to. In motivational interviewing
(MI), a counseling method for behavior change, a turn that seems weak at the time may lead the
patient to argue for change (\emph{change talk}) rather than for the status quo (\emph{sustain
talk}). We present \textbf{\LAGRPO}, which extends look-ahead rewards from preference-tree
optimization to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): before the judge scores a turn, a
patient simulator and the current policy continue the conversation for $K$ turns. We train a
1B-parameter therapist for ten iterations with five-turn look-ahead ($K{=}5$) and without
($K{=}0$). The $K{=}5$ policy leads on all eight MI measures, under the training judge and a
held-out judge. The rewards teach different behavior: the $K{=}0$ policy learns generic praise, even
after sustain talk, while the $K{=}5$ policy learns to reflect change talk, although it argues for
change more often than the untrained model. Under $K{=}5$, change talk is followed by change talk
in $97\%$ of cases, against $80\%$ under $K{=}0$ (training judge).
\end{abstract}
