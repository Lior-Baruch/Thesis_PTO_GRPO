\begin{abstract}
When an LLM judge supplies the reward for a dialogue policy, it is usually shown the candidate
turn and nothing after it. In multi-turn goal-oriented dialogue that is a short-sighted proxy: the
turn that reads best on its own is often not the turn that leads anywhere good. We present
\textbf{\LAGRPO}: before the judge scores a candidate therapist turn, the patient simulator and the
current policy continue the conversation for $K$ further turns, and the judge scores the
continuation. The lever comes from preference-tree optimisation for motivational interviewing
(MI); moved to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), it reweights every sampled candidate
instead of selecting a preference pair. In a controlled pair of ten-iteration runs that differ
only in the horizon, $K{=}5$ leads on the rewarded rubric from the fourth iteration on, beats the
$K{=}0$ policy's best checkpoint as well as its last, leads on all eight evaluation instruments
under both the training oracle and a held-out judge from another model family, and replicates on
a fresh evaluation draw. It also changes what is
learned. Turn-level reward teaches flattery: MI-inconsistent behaviour quadruples over the $K{=}0$
run, almost all of it unearned praise, and a judge-free lexical marker corroborates the coded
rate. Two caveats travel with the result. Under the held-out judge the look-ahead policy's
MI-inconsistency still rises, at under half the turn-level rate; and at the best checkpoint the
training oracle's scores reach the ceiling of its scale, where it loses three-quarters of its
per-conversation variance while the held-out judge, with headroom, loses none. The arm-level
ordering survives both; the per-conversation scores at that checkpoint do not.
\end{abstract}
