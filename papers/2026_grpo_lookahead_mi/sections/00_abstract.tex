\begin{abstract}
In reinforcement learning for dialogue, an LLM judge commonly scores each candidate turn without
seeing what follows it. Yet in counseling, motivational interviewing (MI) judges a therapist's turn
partly by whether the patient then argues for change (\emph{change talk}) or for the status quo
(\emph{sustain talk}). We therefore let the judge see where a turn leads: a patient simulator and
the current policy first continue the conversation for $K$ more turns. Our method,
\textbf{\LAGRPO}, extends this look-ahead reward from preference-tree optimization to Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). We train a 1B-parameter therapist in two otherwise matched
ten-iteration runs, with five turns of look-ahead ($K{=}5$) and with none ($K{=}0$). Under the
training oracle and a held-out judge from another model family, the $K{=}5$ policy ends ahead on
all eight MI instruments, against $K{=}0$'s last and best checkpoints. The two policies also became
different therapists. The $K{=}0$ policy learned non-specific praise, even after sustain talk. The
$K{=}5$ policy learned to reflect change talk, though it met sustain talk with persuasion more
often than the untrained model. Under it, change talk is followed by change talk in $97\%$ of cases
in the average conversation, against $80\%$ under $K{=}0$ (training oracle).
\end{abstract}
