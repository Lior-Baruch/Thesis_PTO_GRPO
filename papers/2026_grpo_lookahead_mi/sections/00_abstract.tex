\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning has improved language models most clearly on verifiable tasks. In
non-verifiable, multi-turn dialogue, the LLM judge that supplies the reward commonly sees nothing
after the candidate turn, whose value often lies in what follows. In motivational interviewing
(MI), a therapist's utterance matters for what the patient says next. We present
\textbf{\LAGRPO}, which extends look-ahead reward from preference-tree optimization to Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): before the judge scores a candidate therapist turn, a patient
simulator and the current policy continue the conversation for $K$ turns, and the judge scores the
extended conversation.
In two ten-iteration runs of a 1B-parameter therapist, one per horizon and otherwise matched,
$K{=}5$ ends ahead of $K{=}0$'s last and reward-selected best checkpoints on all eight MI
instruments, under the training oracle and a held-out judge from another model family. Utterance
coding by both judges shows turn-level reward taught non-specific praise, including after
sustain talk, while look-ahead taught complex reflections of change talk, with more persuasion after
sustain talk than the untrained model. In the average conversation under look-ahead,
patient change talk is followed by change talk in $97\%$ of cases, against $80\%$ without it
(training oracle; held-out judge: $94\%$ and $73\%$).
\end{abstract}
