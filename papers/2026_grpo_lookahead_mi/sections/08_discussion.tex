We asked whether a small therapist model learns motivational interviewing better when the judge
that rewards each of its turns also sees how the conversation continues. In this pair of runs it
did: with the same training method and the same judge, the model trained with a five-turn
look-ahead finished training ahead on every instrument, under both judges, against the other run's
best checkpoint as well as its last. It also became a different kind of therapist.

The difference is clearest in praise. In the middle of training both rewards gave higher scores to
replies that praised the patient; in the last training round only the $\Kz$ reward, which scores a
turn before the patient has answered it, still did (\S\ref{sec:patient}). The $\Kz$ policy ends up
praising in $0.41$ of its turns and in reply to a third of the patient's sustain talk. What the
$\Kf$ run learned instead is to keep the patient's change talk going (\S\ref{sec:patient}).

Look-ahead did not make the policy consistent with MI; it changed which MI-inconsistent habit
the policy learned. Part of what the $\Kf$ policy learned is what MI prescribes: reflecting the
patient's change talk. Part is not: it meets sustain talk with persuasion more often than the Base,
and under the held-out judge its praise also rises above the Base's. We could not isolate how the
horizon produces these differences inside the update (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}); for example,
the $\Kf$ reward agrees only slightly better with the score of the finished session, and no better
at the first iteration, where both runs start from the Base. What we can measure is that the two
rewards prefer different candidates within a group (Appendices~\ref{app:premium}
and~\ref{app:direction}) and that the updates differ in size: in every iteration the $\Kz$ policy
moved further from the policy it started from and had the larger gradients
(Appendix~\ref{app:trainer}).
\label{sec:mechanism}

Both runs optimized \QQ, which rates the session from the patient's side (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Q1
also credits moves that MI uses sparingly: among its examples of good practice are a therapist who
``suggests practical steps \ldots, provides uplifting messages, or encourages perseverance'' and
one who ``provides advice or information that directly relates to'' the patient's challenges
(Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}), so the reward partly credits both habits we document, the $\Kz$
policy's praise and the $\Kf$ policy's persuasion. We did not test whether the horizon changes what
is learned in the same way under a reward built from another instrument, such as the MITI ratings.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
% \dnotedone{cut}{For a group-relative optimiser in multi-turn dialogue, what the judge is shown is a first-order design choice \textasciitilde}
In this pair of runs, with the same optimizer and the same oracle, scoring each candidate turn
together with the five turns that follow it gave a final policy ahead on every instrument, and
changed what kind of therapist the policy became: under $\Kz$, one that praises the patient even
in reply to sustain talk; under $\Kf$, one that reflects the patient's change talk, after which the
patient more often expresses it again, but that meets sustain talk with persuasion.
