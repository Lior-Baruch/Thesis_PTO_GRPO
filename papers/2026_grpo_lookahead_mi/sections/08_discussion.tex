We asked whether a small therapist model learns motivational interviewing better when the judge
that rewards each of its turns also sees how the conversation continues. In this pair of runs it
did: the model trained with a five-turn look-ahead finished training ahead on every instrument, under both judges, against the other run's
best checkpoint as well as its last. It also became a different kind of therapist.

% \dnotedone{rephrased}{[their ?]}
The difference is clearest in praise. Both rewards favored praising replies in the middle of
training: among the eight candidates for a prompt, those with a keyword-marker phrase scored
$0.22$--$0.33$ within-group standard deviations above the others under $\Kz$ and $0.16$--$0.21$
under $\Kf$ (candidates from the models of iterations 4--7). In the last round only the $\Kz$
reward, which scores a turn before the patient has answered it, still did ($0.16$ against $0.04$;
Appendix~\ref{app:premium}), and the $\Kz$ policy ends up praising in $0.41$ of its turns. Look-ahead did not make the policy consistent with MI; it changed which MI-inconsistent
habit the policy learned.

The $\Kf$ policy learned part of what MI prescribes, reflecting the patient's change talk, after
which the patient kept expressing it; but it meets sustain talk with persuasion more often than the
Base, and under the held-out judge its praise also rises above the Base's. We could not isolate how
the horizon produces these differences inside the update (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}): the $\Kf$
reward agrees only slightly better with the finished session's score, while the two rewards prefer
different candidates within a group and the $\Kz$ updates are larger.
\label{sec:mechanism}

Both runs optimized \QQ, which rates the session from the patient's side; Q1 also credits moves
that MI uses sparingly: among its examples of good practice are a therapist who
``suggests practical steps \ldots, provides uplifting messages, or encourages perseverance'' and
one who ``provides advice or information that directly relates to'' the patient's challenges
(Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}), so the reward partly credits both habits we document, the $\Kz$
policy's praise and the $\Kf$ policy's persuasion. We did not test whether the horizon changes what
is learned in the same way under a reward built from another instrument, such as the MITI ratings.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
% \dnotedone{cut}{For a group-relative optimiser in multi-turn dialogue, what the judge is shown is a first-order design choice \textasciitilde}
In this pair of runs, scoring each candidate turn with the five turns that follow it gave
a policy ahead on every instrument and a different therapist, one that reflects change talk instead
of praising, but persuades in reply to sustain talk.
