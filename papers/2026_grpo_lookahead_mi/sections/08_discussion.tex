\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

% \dnotedone{new opening paragraph, no numbers}{starts too specific and too AI.. start with a sentence or 2-3 sentences more high level summary}
% \dnotedone{cut}{\textbf{The horizon of the reward selects which behaviour pays.} i dont link this jargon as well}
We asked whether a small therapist model learns motivational interviewing better when the judge
that rewards each of its turns also sees how the conversation continues. It does: with the same
training method and the same judge, the model trained with a five-turn look-ahead finished
training ahead on every instrument, under both judges. It also became a different kind of
therapist.

% \dnotedone{rephrased}{[paid for non-specific praise \textasciitilde] [stops at the reply \textasciitilde] [paying] [paid]}
% \dnotedone{rephrased}{[their ?]}
The difference is clearest in praise. In the middle of training, both rewards gave higher scores to
replies that praised the patient. Late in training, only the $\Kz$ reward, which scores a turn
before the patient has answered it, still did: among candidates sampled from the iteration-9
model, replies containing a praise phrase scored $0.16$ within-group standard deviations above the candidates for the same
prompt that did not praise, against $0.04$ under the $\Kf$ reward, which also sees the next five
turns (Appendix~\ref{app:premium}). The $\Kz$ policy ends up praising in $0.41$ of its turns and in
reply to a third of the patient's sustain talk, and late in the session its patients express even
less change talk than the Base's. What the $\Kf$ run learned instead is to keep the patient's
change talk going.
% \dnotedone{rephrased}{[again after $97\%$ of their change talk ??]}
When a patient expressed change talk, their next utterance was change talk again in $97\%$ of
cases under $\Kf$, against $80\%$ under $\Kz$ ($75\%$ at the Base).

% \dnotedone{rephrased, one idea per sentence}{- try to rephrase this - there is an interesting story here but need to tell it better}
Look-ahead did not make the policy consistent with MI; it changed which MI-inconsistent habit
the policy learned. Part of
what the $\Kf$ policy learned is what MI prescribes: reflecting the patient's change talk. Part is
not: it meets sustain talk with persuasion, and under the held-out judge its praise also rises
above the Base's. We could not isolate how the horizon produces these differences inside the
update. We tested three explanations and none was confirmed (Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}); for
example, the $\Kf$ reward agrees only slightly better with the score of the finished session, and
no better at the first iteration, where both runs start from the Base. What we can measure is
that the two rewards prefer different candidates within a group
(Appendices~\ref{app:premium} and~\ref{app:direction}) and that the updates differ in size: in
every iteration the $\Kz$ policy moved further from the policy it started from, with gradients
about twice as large (Appendix~\ref{app:trainer}). The utterance codes of
\S\ref{sec:therapist}--\ref{sec:patient} show what these differences add up to.
\label{sec:mechanism}

\dnotedone{the reward choice, and what it leaves open}{need to talk about this - here briefly but also in intro and/or discussion - I guess the excuse is following Yosef et al .. }
Both runs optimized the mean of Q1 and Q2, which we chose because \citet{yosef2024assessing}
built and validated these questionnaires for LLM evaluation of simulated MI sessions. They rate the
session from the patient's side: satisfaction, alliance and relational communication. Whether the horizon changes what is
learned in the same way under a reward built from another instrument, such as the MITI ratings, we
did not test.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
% \dnotedone{cut}{For a group-relative optimiser in multi-turn dialogue, what the judge is shown is a first-order design choice \textasciitilde}
Scoring the continuation rather than single turns raised what the same optimizer extracted from the
same oracle, and it decided what kind of therapist the policy became: one that praises the patient
even when they argue for the status quo, or one that reflects the patient's change talk and hears
more of it, though it still persuades when the patient resists.
