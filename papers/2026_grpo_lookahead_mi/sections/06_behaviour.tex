\section{Behavioural analysis: over-praise under turn-level reward}
\label{sec:behaviour}

A higher score is not by itself evidence of better therapy. Table~\ref{tab:excerpt} illustrates
the behavioural difference between the two rewards that the statistics below quantify.

\textbf{The $\Kz$ policy learns to over-praise.} Under turn-level reward the per-turn rate of
MI-inconsistent behaviour rises from $0.211$ at base to $\mathbf{0.838}$ at iteration 10
($\dz\,1.717$), fourfold, over a run whose \QQ\ score was simultaneously going up. Almost all of it
is one channel: at the endpoint the $\Kz$ policy commits $9.9$ coded MI-inconsistent acts per
session, of which $8.3$ ($84\%$) are \textbf{over-praise}, generic affirmation of whatever the
patient just said. The distinction is MI's own: the MITI manual codes an affirmation only when it
is specific to a client strength or effort, excludes ``cheerleading'' and non-specific praise, and
lists ``I am really proud of you'' as not coded \citep{moyers2016miti}, almost word for word the
$\Kz$ turn in Table~\ref{tab:excerpt}. Under look-ahead the per-turn rate is
unchanged under the training oracle ($0.209 \rightarrow 0.210$, $\dz\,0.006$, n.s.), and the
endpoint session holds $2.9$ MI-inconsistent acts against the base's $2.7$, with the base's
composition; the held-out judge reads the same conversations differently, as discussed below. The
mechanism is in the rubric. Q1 and Q2 ask a simulated patient how satisfied they were and how
strong the alliance felt; praising the patient raises those ratings \emph{in the turn where it
happens}, and the cost, a session that never reaches change talk, lands several turns later,
precisely the horizon a $K{=}0$ reward cannot see and a $K{=}5$ reward can.
The change-talk instrument registers that cost: at the endpoint the $\Kf$ policy's patients
produce a higher share of change talk under both graders (PCT $+0.111$, $\dz\,0.516$; $+0.113$,
$\dz\,0.563$; Table~\ref{tab:endpoint}).

\textbf{Corroboration without a grader.} An MI-inconsistency count is itself produced by an
LLM, so the finding above could in principle be a grader artifact. A \textbf{deterministic lexical
over-praise marker}, ten keyword patterns matched against the transcript
(Appendix~\ref{app:marker}) and therefore identical under both graders, gives the share of
therapist turns containing at least one such marker. Under $\Kf$ that share never exceeds
$0.064$; under $\Kz$ it climbs late and steeply, $0.275$ at iteration 8, $0.093$ at 9, and
$\mathbf{0.671}$ at iteration 10. The oracle-coded over-praise rate on the same conversations
agrees, $0.698$ against $0.051$ at the endpoint, and is significantly higher under $\Kz$ at every
iteration from the fifth on under the training oracle and from the fourth under the held-out judge
(Figure~\ref{fig:overpraise}). The marker is deliberately brittle; the agreement between the two
carries the argument, and its onset matches the reward gap's (\S\ref{sec:reward}), as a common
cause predicts. The size of the final jump is one iteration of one run; the separation from
iteration 5 on is the robust part of the claim.

\textbf{What look-ahead does instead.} The $\Kf$ policy asks \textbf{more questions per therapist
turn}, a text count, significantly so at 7 of 10 iterations (mean $\dz\,0.643$), and makes fewer
affirmations per turn ($\dz\,0.75$): the direction MI training is meant to move a counsellor. The
full channel profile (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:forest}) also shows a smaller residue in the other
direction: at the endpoint the $\Kf$ policy is coded as more directive, with more direct/order
($\dz\,0.60$) and persuasion ($\dz\,0.52$) per turn, both MI-inconsistent. Look-ahead removes
the dominant channel, and part of what it selects is a therapist who pushes: MI's \emph{righting
reflex}, the counsellor's urge to correct and persuade \citep{miller2013mi}, also the failure mode
\citet{chiu2024bolt} report for LLM therapists. The $\Kf$ endpoint sessions are longer ($31.9$
vs.\ $25.2$ turns, $\dz\,0.43$), consistent with the keep-the-session-open pressure of the
rollout (Limitations).

\textbf{Under the held-out judge.}
\label{sec:behaviour-heldout}
That look-ahead prevents the over-praise drift holds only under the training oracle. The held-out
judge reads the same $\Kf$ conversations as rising from $0.326$ to $0.628$ MI-inconsistent acts
per turn ($\dz\,0.845$). What survives both graders is the \emph{comparison}: held out, $\Kf$'s
rise is $0.301$ against $\Kz$'s $0.666$, under half the rate, and the endpoint contrast is
$\dz\,-1.567$ in $\Kf$'s favour. Trajectory-level reward thus \textbf{slows the drift to under
half its turn-level rate and removes its dominant channel}; \S\ref{sec:measurement} explains why
the graders disagree at exactly this checkpoint.
