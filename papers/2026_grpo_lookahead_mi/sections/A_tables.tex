\section{The mechanism analysis in full}
\label{app:mechanism}

This appendix expands \S\ref{sec:mechanism}.

\subsection{The faithfulness statistic}

The training reward scores a \emph{partial} conversation; the evaluation scores the whole session.
The gap between them is measurable as pairwise rank agreement: among candidate turns compared
within one (arm, iteration, conversation-length) cell, how often does the training reward order
them the way the full-session score eventually does? Chance is $0.5$; intervals are
conversation-level cluster bootstraps.

Pooled over the ten iterations both arms reached, agreement is $0.909$ $[0.900, 0.917]$ under $\Kf$
against $0.873$ $[0.861, 0.884]$ under $\Kz$ on the training oracle, and $0.800$ against $0.747$ on
the held-out judge; both differences have intervals excluding zero, and $\Kf$ is the more faithful
arm at 7 of 10 iterations under both graders. Two qualifications travel with that number. First,
the iteration-level Wilcoxon test --- with the ten iterations, rather than the branch pairs, as the
unit of observation --- does \emph{not} clear $.05$ ($p = .084$ primary, $p = .193$ held out). The
pooled interval treats branch pairs as independent and is the more permissive of the two, so we
describe the difference as small and consistent rather than significant. Second, at iteration $n$
the two arms are \emph{different policies} generating \emph{different conversations}, so this
comparison measures a joint property of the scoring rule and of the trajectory the scoring rule put
the policy on; it cannot separate them.

\subsection{At a matched policy, the effect disappears}

There is exactly one cut in this design where the scoring rule is close to the only difference: the
first iteration, where both arms start from the same base policy and differ only in whether five
simulated turns were appended before the oracle saw each candidate. At that cut, nothing is
detectable. All four deltas straddle zero (training oracle: $+0.015$ $[-0.026, 0.059]$ for GRPO;
held-out judge: $-0.014$ $[-0.075, 0.048]$), and the per-length-bin breakdown points in
\emph{opposite directions on the two graders} --- 17 of 20 bins favour $\Kz$ under the training
oracle, 17 of 20 favour $\Kf$ under the held-out judge. A sign that flips with the grader is the
shape of no effect, not of a small one. The matched-policy cut is also the weakest-powered one ---
a single model state per arm, and only approximately matched, since the policy drifts within the
iteration under $K$-specific rewards --- so this is an absence of evidence rather than evidence of
absence. It is not, however, a result we can quietly omit while quoting the pooled row.

\subsection{Dispersion, and the iteration-10 inversion}

The mean best-minus-worst margin within a group is $1.300\times$ larger under $\Kf$
$([1.275, 1.326])$; the within-group standard deviation grows by $1.293\times$ $([1.267, 1.317])$;
the ratio of the two ratios is $1.006$ $([1.002, 1.010])$ --- a pure rescaling, which the advantage
normalisation absorbs.

One iteration inverts the \emph{level} of this. At training iteration 10 the margin ratio drops to
$0.679$ --- $\Kf$'s within-group margins are smaller than $\Kz$'s there. Both sides move: $\Kz$'s
mean margin jumps from $0.248$ to $0.339$ while $\Kf$'s falls from $0.268$ to $0.230$. The $\Kz$
jump coincides exactly with the iteration in which its over-praise rate explodes
(\S\ref{sec:behaviour}: the judge-free marker goes from $0.093$ to $0.671$ between iterations 9 and
10), which is what a group containing both flattering and non-flattering candidates would do to a
grader that rewards flattery --- the pool splits and the best--worst gap widens. The $\Kf$ decline
is the mirror image: a converging policy whose eight samples the oracle scores increasingly alike.
Crucially this does \emph{not} break the rescaling result: at that same iteration the
ratio-of-ratios is $0.964$ --- margin and standard deviation still move together. What inverts is
the level of dispersion in each arm, not the coupling the advantage normalisation absorbs. We offer
the over-praise account as consistent with the timing, not as demonstrated: a single iteration, and
no manipulation separates the two.

\subsection{The update-direction proxy}

Writing each iteration's update direction as the advantage-weighted sum of its candidates' sentence
embeddings, $\operatorname{normalise}(\sum_g w_g \cdot \mathrm{emb}(t_g))$ with $w_g$ the
standardised group-relative advantage that scales each completion's gradient, the two arms' pooled
directions have a cosine of $0.804$ --- $0.851$ once divided by the attenuation ceiling that two
split-half-noisy estimates can reach at all. This is an \emph{embedding-space proxy}, not the
gradient: it measures the semantic direction of what the update prefers, and it says the $\Kz$ and
$\Kf$ policies are pushed along substantially the same direction.
