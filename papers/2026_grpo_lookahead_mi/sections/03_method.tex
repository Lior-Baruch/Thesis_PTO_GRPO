\section{GRPO with look-ahead}
\label{sec:method}

Throughout, a \emph{turn} is one utterance by either speaker, a \emph{prefix} $c$ is a transcript
ending on a patient turn, $\oplus$ appends turns to a transcript, and $\pi_n$ is the therapist
policy at the start of training iteration~$n$. The patient simulator $P$ and the oracle $O$ are
fixed LLMs; $O$ returns a scalar MI score for a transcript.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.84\columnwidth]{figures/method_grpo_group.png}
\caption{One GRPO group under look-ahead. The policy samples $G{=}8$ completions of a prompt; each
is rolled forward $K$ turns by the patient simulator and the current policy; the oracle scores the
extended transcript; the scores are standardised within the group and every completion carries a
gradient. $K{=}0$ removes the rollout and recovers standard GRPO.}
\label{fig:schematic}
\end{figure}

\textbf{The iterative loop.} Training is iterative self-play. At iteration $n$, $\pi_n$ holds 96
conversations with $P$, one per patient persona; each is sliced after every patient turn whose
prefix has at least MCL utterances (MCL${=}12$ here) to give the training prompts. GRPO updates
$\pi_n$ on those prompts for two epochs, the adapter is saved as $\pi_{n+1}$, and the loop repeats
with fresh conversations. The conversations generated at the start of iteration $n$ double as the
evaluation set of $\pi_n$; a generate-only pass after the last update evaluates the final policy,
so ten iterations yield eleven evaluated model states per run.

\textbf{The group.} For each prompt $c$, GRPO samples $G{=}8$ completions $t_1,\dots,t_G$ from the
current policy, scores each with a reward $r_g$, and forms the group-relative advantage
$A_g = (r_g - \operatorname{mean}_g r)/\operatorname{std}_g r$. The update is the PPO-clipped policy
gradient on $A_g$ with a KL penalty toward the iteration-start policy; every completion contributes
a gradient, weighted by how it ranks against its seven siblings.

\textbf{The look-ahead reward.} The only thing we change is the transcript the oracle scores
(Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}). Let $\tau_K(x)$ be the $K$-turn rollout of a transcript $x$: $P$
replies, then $\pi_n$, then $P$, and so on for $K$ turns, stopping early if the patient closes the
session; $\tau_0(x)$ is empty. Then
\[
r_g \;=\; O\big(c \oplus t_g \oplus \tau_K(c \oplus t_g)\big).
\]
At $K{=}5$ the oracle scores the candidate together with the five turns that followed it under the
current policy, so a candidate is credited for where it leads under the therapist that will
actually follow it. The loss, the advantage, the KL penalty, the prompts, and the number of gradient
steps are identical across $K$; the horizon changes only the evidence on which a candidate is ranked
against its siblings. Where PTO used the look-ahead scores to pick one chosen and one rejected turn
per branch point, all eight scores enter the update here. The lever is not free: each $K{=}5$
reward adds three patient-simulator calls and two policy generations to the single oracle call
both arms share, so oracle calls are matched across $K$ by construction while the look-ahead run
makes about $393$k extra patient calls and its optimiser steps take a median $1.92\times$ as long
(Limitations). All comparisons are at matched iteration count, not matched cost.
