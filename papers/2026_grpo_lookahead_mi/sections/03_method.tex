\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Task, simulator and oracle}
\label{sec:task}

A session is a text conversation between a therapist policy and
a simulated patient who is ambivalent about a health behaviour. The patient is simulated by
\texttt{gpt-4o-mini} conditioned on one of \textbf{96} persona system prompts [ref some kfir paper?], the exhaustive
enumeration of $2\ \text{gender} \times 3\ \text{cooperation} \times 2\ \text{problem} \times
2\ \text{duration} \times 2\ \text{prior attempts} \times 2\ \text{age}$
(Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). A session runs until the patient closes it or a cap of 49 utterances
after the scripted opening is reached; most close earlier, and base-policy sessions average 28--29
utterances. The therapist is based on \texttt{Llama-3.2-1B} under a fixed counsellor system prompt,
trained with LoRA adapters, responses capped at 200 tokens (all setting parameters in
Table~\ref{tab:config}). The oracle is the same \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} with JSON-schema-constrained
output; the \emph{training} reward is the mean of Q1 and Q2 below, matching \citet{baruch2025pto}. [need to talk about this - here briefly but also in intro and/or discussion - I guess the excuse is following Yosef et al .. ]

\textbf{Why MI.} MI's constructs are defined by the patient's response rather than by the
counsellor's utterance: its target outcome is \emph{change talk}, the patient's own statements in
favour of change, as against \emph{sustain talk}, and the MITI coding scheme rates a session on
cultivating change talk and softening sustain talk, properties of the trajectory
rather than of any one turn \citep{miller2013mi, moyers2016miti}. The value of a therapist turn is
therefore inherently delayed, which is what a reward horizon is meant to capture, and the field
supplies validated instruments and a tradition of coding every utterance and asking what follows
it.

\subsection{GRPO with look-ahead}
\label{sec:lagrpo}

Throughout, a \emph{turn} is one utterance by either speaker, a \emph{prefix} $c$ is a transcript
ending on a patient turn, $\oplus$ appends turns to a transcript, $\pi_n$ is the therapist policy
at the start of training iteration~$n$ ($\pi_1$ is the base model), and $\pi$ is the policy being
updated within an iteration ($\pi = \pi_n$ at its start). The patient simulator $P$ and the oracle
$O$ are fixed LLMs; $O$ returns a scalar MI score for a transcript. Algorithm~\ref{alg:loop}
states the procedure; Figure~\ref{fig:schematic} shows one group.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/method_grpo_group.png}
\caption{One GRPO group under look-ahead. The policy samples $G{=}8$ completions of a prompt; each
is rolled forward $K$ turns by the patient simulator $P$ and the policy $\pi$ being trained; the
oracle scores the extended transcript; the scores are standardised within the group and every
completion carries a gradient. At $K{=}0$ the rollout is skipped, the oracle scores the candidate
alone, and standard GRPO is recovered.}
\label{fig:schematic}
\end{figure*}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\LAGRPO\ (one arm)}
\label{alg:loop}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE base policy $\pi_1$; simulator $P$; oracle $O$; horizon $K$; group size $G$; MCL;
  iterations $N$
\FOR{$n = 1, \dots, N$}
  \STATE $\mathcal{C} \gets$ 96 conversations of $\pi_n$ with $P$ \COMMENT{eval set of $\pi_n$}
  \STATE $\mathcal{D} \gets$ patient-ending prefixes of $\mathcal{C}$ with $\geq$ MCL turns
  \STATE $\pi \gets \pi_n$
  \FOR{each $c \in \mathcal{D}$ (two epochs)}
    \STATE $t_1, \dots, t_G \sim \pi(\cdot \mid c)$
    \STATE $r_g \gets O\big(c \oplus t_g \oplus \tau_K(c \oplus t_g)\big)$ for all $g$
      \COMMENT{Eq.~\ref{eq:reward}}
    \STATE $A_g \gets (r_g - \operatorname{mean}_g r)/\operatorname{std}_g r$
    \STATE one step on Eq.~\ref{eq:grpo} over $\{(c, t_g, A_g)\}_g$, KL to $\pi_n$
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $\pi_{n+1} \gets \pi$
\ENDFOR
\STATE 96 conversations of $\pi_{N+1}$ with $P$ \COMMENT{eval set of $\pi_{N+1}$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{The iterative loop.} At iteration $n$, $\pi_n$ holds 96 conversations with $P$, one per
patient persona; each is sliced after every patient turn whose prefix has at least MCL utterances
(the \emph{minimum context length}, 12 here; see below) to form the training prompts. For each
prompt GRPO samples $G{=}8$ completions, scores them, and takes one step on the group-standardised
scores; after two epochs the policy is saved as $\pi_{n+1}$ and the loop repeats with fresh
conversations. The conversations generated at the
start of iteration $n$ double as the evaluation set of $\pi_n$; a generate-only pass after the
last update evaluates the final policy, so ten iterations yield eleven evaluated model states per
run.

\textbf{The look-ahead reward.} The only thing we change [from what??] is the transcript the oracle scores.
Let $\tau_K(x)$ be the $K$-turn rollout of a transcript $x$: $P$ replies, then $\pi$, then $P$,
and so on for $K$ turns, stopping early if the patient closes the session; $\tau_0(x)$ is empty.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reward}
r_g \;=\; O\big(c \oplus t_g \oplus \tau_K(c \oplus t_g)\big).
\end{equation}
At $K{=}5$ the oracle scores the candidate together with the five turns that followed it under the
current policy, so a candidate is credited for where it leads under the therapist that will
actually follow it, [at the price of three [why? is this important?] patient-simulator calls and two policy generations per
candidate and roughly twice the per-step wall-clock (comparisons are at matched iteration count;
the Limitations give the matched-cost reading). this all seems too AI. rephrase to make it clear and focus on what is interesting..]

\textbf{The update.} The step maximises the GRPO objective of \citet{shao2024deepseekmath}: the
per-token surrogate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:grpo}
\ell_{g,i} = \min(\rho_{g,i} A_g,\,\bar{\rho}_{g,i} A_g) - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}_{g,i},
\end{equation}
averaged over the tokens of each completion and then over the group, where $\rho_{g,i}$ is the
$i$-th token's probability ratio between $\pi$ and the sampling policy, $\bar{\rho}_{g,i}$ its
clip to $[1{-}\epsilon, 1{+}\epsilon]$, and $\mathrm{KL}_{g,i}$ the per-token KL estimate to
$\pi_n$. Both arms make one update per sampled batch
(Table~\ref{tab:config}), so $\rho_{g,i}{=}1$ at the update, the clipping is inactive, and the
step is the advantage-weighted policy gradient over the group with the KL penalty. The loss, the
advantage normalisation, the KL penalty and the prompt-construction rule are identical across
$K$; the horizon enters only through $r_g$.

\textbf{Why the transfer is not trivial.} [which transfer? ] A rollout is one sample from the distribution of
continuations, so $r_g$ is a one-sample Monte Carlo estimate of the candidate's downstream value,
with sampling noise from $P$ and $\pi$ added to the oracle's own. PTO filters that noise by
keeping only the best- and worst-scoring candidates, so an unlucky rollout can at most cost a
pair; GRPO trains on all $G$ candidates, so the rollout variance enters every advantage.

\textbf{Minimum context length.} Training prompts are prefixes, so the oracle scores a partial
conversation while the evaluation scores a whole session. In a pilot on an earlier configuration
of this task the oracle's ranking of very short prefixes agreed poorly with its ranking of the
completed sessions and recovered from roughly ten utterances, so both arms discard prefixes
shorter than MCL${=}12$; at that minimum the training reward orders candidates as the
full-session score does in $86$--$89\%$ of pairs, and agreement does not fall with prefix length
(Appendix~\ref{app:mechanism}). [ref specific subsection and related figure]

\subsection{Evaluation design}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Instruments.} Evaluation uses eight instruments, each administered by a grader LLM shown
the full transcript (items and prompts in Appendix~\ref{app:instruments}). Q1 and Q2 are the
LLM-evaluator questionnaires of \citet{yosef2024assessing}, 5 and 17 patient-perspective Likert
items on session satisfaction and on alliance and relational communication, shown there to
separate levels of therapist expertise. WAI-SR \citep{hatcher2006waisr} and
CSQ-8 \citep{larsen1979csq} are published patient-report instruments, and MI-SAT is a six-item
intervention-satisfaction survey adapted for this task. Three coders count behaviour: a
MITI~4.2.1-style coder \citep{moyers2016miti} whose four global ratings are averaged; a patient
change-talk coder (PCT; the share of change talk among change and sustain talk, the patient-side
outcome MI aims at); and an MI-inconsistent-behaviour coder (MICI; coded acts per therapist turn,
lower is better). \QQ\ is thus both the optimisation target and a reported outcome
(Limitations), which is why a held-out grader is central here.

\textbf{The process coder.} The eight instruments are session-level aggregates. For
\S\ref{sec:therapist}--\ref{sec:patient} a fourth coder assigns one code to \emph{every}
therapist utterance, its dominant function in MITI and MISC terms (open question, closed
question, simple reflection, complex reflection, affirmation, non-specific praise, giving
information, persuasion, seeking collaboration, confront, other), and one code to every patient
utterance (change talk, sustain talk, neutral), in transcript order (Appendix~\ref{app:coder}).
Affirmation follows the MITI definition, a comment on a specific client strength or effort, and
non-specific praise is coded separately, the distinction the MITI manual draws when it lists ``I
am really proud of you'' as not an affirmation. Because the codes are positional, they [say what
the patient said next ?~] and how a session develops.

\textbf{Terminology.} The two scoring models are the \textbf{training oracle} (\primaryjudge,
which also plays the patient) and the \textbf{held-out judge} (\heldoutjudge, a different model
family that never touched training); \emph{grader} refers to either, and each administers the
eight instruments and the process coder.

\textbf{The two arms.} The runs are a controlled pair: their tracked run configurations differ in
exactly two fields, the look-ahead depth and its batching size (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
Everything else, from $G{=}8$, KL $\beta{=}0.01$ and the sampling temperature to the seed, the
oracle and the persona set, is identical (Table~\ref{tab:config}).

\textbf{Evaluation and statistics.} Every model state is scored on all eight instruments and
coded utterance by utterance for all 96 personas by both graders. All contrasts are
\textbf{persona-paired} (personas are reshuffled every iteration, so pairing is on the recovered
persona identity), with Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Cohen's \dz, bootstrap intervals, and Holm
correction for multiple comparisons within each family of tests. Grader \emph{levels} are not comparable (on \QQ\ the
held-out judge is harsher by 1.1--1.8 points), so only contrasts cross graders and the two are
never averaged.
