# Review of *GRPO with Look-Ahead in Motivational Interviewing* (draft of 2026-09-14)

Scope of this read: `main.tex`, all sixteen section files, `README.md`, `NUMBERS.md`, the compiled
`main.pdf` (21 pages; body ends at the bottom of page 8), and spot checks of the quoted numbers
against `k_endpoints.md`, `multijudge_sign_preservation_grpo.md` and `sd_by_iter.md`. Goal, as you
put it: clean it up, make it read professionally for the supervisors, and keep it a paper about
**GRPO with look-ahead** and **motivational interviewing**.

## Verdict in short

The science is in good shape and the numbers are trustworthy: every figure I re-checked matched its
table, and the ledger shows two independent audits already ran. The controlled pair, the
persona-paired statistics, the two graders, the replicate draw and the best-checkpoint comparison
are exactly what a careful reviewer wants to see. What the draft needs before the supervisors read
it is not more evidence but a change of **proportion and voice**:

1. The paper's named contribution, the method, gets about 350 words of prose (§3), while the
   grader-saturation analysis (§8) plus the null mechanism results (§7) take roughly 740 words,
   a full-width three-panel figure, and a third of the abstract. The paper is currently as much
   about *LLM-judge measurement* as about GRPO with look-ahead in MI.
2. The prose hedges constantly and in an aphoristic register ("what changed is the ruler",
   "the move is not obviously safe", "the defensible sentence is"). It is honest, which is right,
   but the caveats are spread over the abstract, intro, §6, §8, §9 and eleven Limitations
   paragraphs, so the reader loses the main message. Consolidate them and neutralise the voice.

Below: the structural changes (A), the register pass (B), section-by-section notes (C), a
consistency checklist (D), questions the supervisors will ask (E), and mechanical fixes (F).

### A2. Demote saturation from a headline contribution to a robustness finding

Contribution (iii) and the "worth naming" paragraph in §9 make grader saturation a co-equal claim.
It is a good finding, but it competes with the main story and, placed at the end of the abstract
and intro, it makes your own headline number look untrustworthy just as the reader has absorbed
it. I would keep the analysis (a reviewer who spots it themselves would be worse), but frame it
as: *the held-out judge is what makes the arm-level result safe; here is why that matters at the
winning checkpoint*. Rewrite (iii) to one clause inside (i), or make it a secondary "we also
document" contribution.

You made the "ceiling-driven" reframing deliberately on 09-14, so treat this as a recommendation
to raise with the supervisors rather than a mandate. One good way to phrase the question to them:
"Is the saturation finding a contribution of this paper or a robustness check of the main one?"

### A3. Let the abstract end on the result

At present about a third of the abstract is caveats and the last sentence is "the per-conversation
scores at that checkpoint do not". Suggested shape (still ~200 words): problem (turn-only judging
is short-sighted in MI) → method (GRPO with a K-turn look-ahead reward; lever from PTO, moved to a
group-relative optimiser) → design (controlled pair, ten iterations, eight instruments, two
graders) → result (leads from iteration 4, on all instruments, beats K=0's best checkpoint on the
rewarded rubric, replicates) → MI finding (turn-level reward induces over-praise, an
MI-inconsistent behaviour, four-fold; look-ahead slows the drift to under half that rate and the
policy asks questions instead) → one sentence of caveat (single training run per arm; the
training oracle saturates at the winning checkpoint, so the held-out judge carries the
per-conversation claims).

### A4. Consolidate the Limitations

Eleven paragraphs over more than a page, several repeating body content. A supervisor-friendly set of
six: (1) one training run per arm; (2) evaluation draws and the replicate, one sentence; (3)
matched iterations, not matched cost; (4) K ∈ {0, 5} only; (5) simulation only: in-sample
personas, patient and oracle share a model, no human coder (merge three paragraphs); (6)
instruments: the reward is a reported outcome, MITI is least dependable, no per-channel
reliability (merge two). The rollout-audit paragraph is a *result*, not a limitation: its numbers
already live in the Figure 8 caption; leave one sentence here ("the look-ahead reward carries a
mild pressure to keep the session open; Appendix A") and move the detail to Appendix A's text.
The response-cap paragraph can be one sentence in (5) or in Appendix D's preamble, where it
already appears.

### A5. Give the MI domain its vocabulary

The paper already contains the MI content; it just does not name it as MI would.

- **"Flattery"** appears four times in the body, including as the §6 title (and twice more in
  Appendix B). The coded construct is *over-praise*, an MI-inconsistent behaviour; MI
  distinguishes an *affirmation* (specific, about a client strength or effort) from generic
  praise. Use "over-praise" and "unearned affirmation" in titles and claims, keep "flattery" for
  at most one sentence of colour. Check MITI 4.2.1's Affirm definition; if it excludes generic
  praise, cite it in §6, because it grounds the MICI over-praise code in the coding manual rather
  than in your own rubric.
- **The directive residue** (more direct/order and persuasion under K=5) is what MI calls the
  *righting reflex* (Miller & Rollnick 2013): the counsellor's urge to fix and persuade. Naming
  it in §6 and §9 connects your finding to MI theory and to Chiu et al.'s BOLT result in one
  stroke, and it turns "part of what it selects is a therapist who pushes" into a recognisable
  clinical construct.
- **Change talk is the MI outcome, and §6 never quotes it.** §6's central mechanism sentence
  says the cost of over-praise is "a session that never reaches change talk", yet the section
  never cites the PCT contrast that sits in Table 1 (K=5 higher on the change-talk share under
  both graders, $d_z$ 0.52 / 0.56). One sentence closes the loop and makes the MI argument
  evidential rather than narrative. In §4, when PCT is defined, add that it is the patient-side
  outcome MI aims at, which is what makes it the most important non-reward instrument.
- Keep the therapist/patient vocabulary (MI uses *client*; your simulator prompts say
  *patient*, so *patient* is fine, but say once that you follow the prompts).

## B. Register: from lab notebook to journal prose

The draft has a distinctive voice: short declaratives, aphorisms, meta-commentary about the
authors' own honesty. Supervisors and ARR reviewers tend to read that as over-stylised. A pass to
a neutral scientific register will make it read as finished. Concrete instances, with a
replacement:

| Where | Now | Suggest |
|---|---|---|
| §1 ¶3 | "The move is not obviously safe." … "is the question." | "The transfer is not trivial: …" … "We ask whether the look-ahead reward retains its benefit under group-relative optimisation and how it shapes the policy over ten iterations." |
| §1 ¶2 | "A judge shown only the turn sees none of this. It sees … exactly the signal a policy can capture by flattering them." | This is the hypothesis §6 then tests. Write it as one: "We hypothesise that a turn-level judge rewards what is locally visible, above all whether the patient felt validated, a signal a policy can exploit with unearned praise." |
| §4 | "a controlled pair, checkable rather than asserted" | delete the aside; "differ in exactly two tracked fields" already makes the point. |
| §5 | "so the gain is not an artifact of the optimisation target"; "which separates a genuine gain from a target-specific one" | both over-claim from a larger dz. "consistent with a gain that is not specific to the optimisation target". |
| §6 | "shows what the two rewards teach the policy to do before any statistic does" | "illustrates the behavioural difference the statistics below quantify". |
| §6 | "The defensible sentence is that …" | "The claim that survives both graders is that …" |
| §6 | "it affirms less ($d_z$ 0.75 fewer affirmations per turn)" | an effect size is not a count: "makes fewer affirmations per turn ($d_z$ 0.75)". |
| §8 | "what changed is the ruler, because the optimisation succeeded on the ruler's own terms" | "the change is in the measurement instrument rather than in the conversations: optimisation against the oracle succeeded on the oracle's own scale." |
| §1 ¶5 | "the arm-level conclusion survives while the per-conversation ruler does not" | "the arm-level conclusion survives while the training oracle's per-conversation scores do not". |
| §9 | "Extending the horizon did not make the policy honest; it made the dishonest strategy unprofitable." | "Extending the horizon does not remove reward hacking; it changes which behaviour is rewarded, because the deferred cost of over-praise now falls inside the scored window." |
| §9 | "What would change our minds: …" | "Evidence that would overturn the conclusion: …" or move to Limitations. |
| §9 | "at the checkpoint a practitioner would ship" | "at the checkpoint that would be selected for deployment". |
| §9 | "Report the reward horizon as a hyperparameter, and watch the training grader's variance as a statistic." | "We recommend reporting the reward horizon as a hyperparameter and monitoring the training grader's per-conversation variance alongside its mean." |
| Lim. | "This is the limitation a reader should weight most heavily." | "We regard this as the principal limitation." |
| App. B | "A sign that flips with the grader is the shape of no effect." / "It is not, however, a result we can omit while quoting the pooled row." | "An effect whose sign depends on the grader is not distinguishable from zero." / delete the second sentence. |
| App. C | "the single easiest way to get these numbers wrong" | "would pair unrelated conversations". |
| App. C | "so a grader is an ordinary partition key rather than a privileged path" | delete; implementation detail. |
| passim | "(§6 says why)", "§8 explains why …" | "(see §6)". |

**Section titles.** The results sections carry claim-style titles ("Look-ahead leads on every
instrument and keeps the lead", "Turn-level reward teaches flattery", "Why it works, as far as we
can tell", "Where the training grader stops discriminating") while §1–§4 are conventional. Pick
one convention. For a supervisor read I would go conventional and put the claim in the first
sentence: "Results on the rewarded rubric and the evaluation instruments", "Behavioural
analysis: over-praise under turn-level reward", "Mechanism analysis", "Saturation of the training
oracle at the winning checkpoint". If you keep claim titles, at least make "as far as we can tell"
go.

**Bold run-in paragraph heads** are fine in ACL style, but several are full sentences with
commas ("The effect has an onset, and $K{=}0$ does not hold what it gains."). Shorten to noun
phrases ("Onset and the $K{=}0$ decline.").

## C. Section-by-section

**Abstract.** See A3. Also: "beats the $K{=}0$ policy's best checkpoint as well as its last" is
unqualified; your own ledger warns the steelman holds on Q1+Q2 only (MICI is *worse* against
LA0-I3 held-out). Add "on the rewarded rubric". Same sentence in §1 ¶5. And "five-turn
continuation" will be read by many as five exchanges; say once, here or in §3, that a turn is one
utterance, so K=5 is patient–therapist–patient–therapist–patient.

**§1 Introduction.** Strong MI opening. ¶3's variance argument belongs in §3 (keep one sentence
here). ¶5 restates §5 with numbers ("1.3 to 2.6 times") and then the contributions restate ¶5;
one of the three should go. Contribution (i) is a 35-word clause; aim for 20. Contribution (ii)
is framed as a measurement point ("a deterministic transcript statistic, not a model judge");
frame it in MI terms: "turn-level reward induces over-praise, an MI-inconsistent behaviour;
look-ahead prevents it under the training oracle and halves its rate under a held-out judge,
corroborated by a judge-free lexical marker".

**§2 Related work.** Good coverage after the 09-14 additions. "now the most widely used
optimiser for RL on language models" is unverifiable; "widely used". The last sentence has a
dangling appositive ("…by advising where a counsellor would reflect (Chiu et al.), the directive
residue we find under look-ahead"); make it a clause: "a pattern that recurs, at a smaller scale,
in our look-ahead policy (§6)". Consider one sentence on the *righting reflex* here or in §6.

**§3 Method.** The core of the paper and currently the thinnest section. Beyond A1: `MCL` is
used before it is defined (expand "minimum context length (MCL)" on first use); "the adapter is
saved as $\pi_{n+1}$" mentions a LoRA adapter before LoRA is introduced in §4 (say "the updated
policy"); "chosen from a pilot in which shorter prefixes were scored unreliably" is vague where
you have a number. Figure 1 is now legible and good; it shows one group but not the outer
iterative loop, which is what makes conversations double as the evaluation set; an Algorithm box
fixes that without redrawing. Small: the update box reads "$\sum_g A_g \nabla\log\pi + \beta\,
\mathrm{KL}$", which mixes a gradient with a penalty; "maximise $\sum_g A_g \log\pi(t_g|c) -
\beta\,\mathrm{KL}$" is cleaner.

**§4 Setup.** The first sentence opens with LoRA rank, learning rate and seed; move those to a
pointer at Table 5 and let the paragraph read task → patients → therapist → oracle. Add the MI
grounding (A1, A5). Add a two-line terminology note: *training oracle* (gpt-4o-mini; also the
patient simulator), *held-out judge* (Claude Haiku 4.5), *instruments* (the eight), *coders* (the
three behaviour-count instruments). "grader" appears 58 times in the body as a generic; with
the note in place you can use the two specific names almost everywhere.

**§5 Results.** The best section: Table 1 in the body was the right call, the best-checkpoint
comparison and the replicate are convincing. Two softenings (B, rows 4–5). "the $\Kz$ arm peaks
at iteration 8 (4.082) and falls to 3.753" is the kind of one-run event Limitations flags; add
"in this run" here too. The replicate paragraph could lose its last two sentences (Limitations
repeats them).

**§6 Behaviour.** The MI heart of the paper and it reads well. Table 2 (the clear case, labelled
as such) is effective. Fixes: A5 vocabulary; "reads 0.209 → 0.210" → "is unchanged (0.209 →
0.210, $d_z$ 0.006, n.s.)"; the parenthetical "(the held-out judge disagrees; see below)"
interrupts the sentence, integrate it; rename `\label{sec:behaviour-honest}` to
`sec:behaviour-heldout` (labels leak into shared source). The mechanism sentence ("Q1 and Q2 ask
a simulated patient how satisfied they were … the cost … lands several turns later") is the
paper's clearest MI argument; consider promoting it to the section's opening rather than the
middle of ¶2.

**§7 Mechanism.** See A1. If kept in the body, lead with the one positive sentence you have
("look-ahead changes *which* candidate the group prefers, not how confidently") and then list
the three tests in one sentence each with a pointer to Appendix B, without the CIs.

**§8 Measurement.** See A1–A2. If shortened, the paragraph to keep whole is "The mechanism is a
ceiling, and only one grader is at it"; it is the one with a mechanism rather than a
description.

**§9 Discussion.** Good content. The "Scope" paragraph handles PTO correctly for the paper, but
see E1 for what the supervisors will ask. Fold §7's residue here if you take that option.

**Limitations / Ethics.** See A4. The Ethics statement is well judged; its "narrow population"
paragraph overlaps the in-sample Limitations paragraph, one cross-reference suffices.

**Appendix B–C.** Written in the internal-notes voice more than the body (B rows in table above).
C.1's Table 5: "arm A / arm B" appear nowhere else (use $K{=}0$ / $K{=}5$); "Eval split 0.05"
and "GRPO loss type `grpo`" are unexplained to a reader (drop or gloss); "Session target 49

## E. Questions the supervisors will ask (prepare answers in the cover note, not the paper)

1. **Why GRPO only, when the 2×2 exists and they signed off on it on 08-27?** The complete grid
   shows the K lever is null-to-negative for PTO, so the interaction is a crossover; that is a
   different and larger paper. Say explicitly what happens to the 2×2 (thesis chapter; a later
   paper), and that PTO appears here as prior work by design. The draft's "outside this paper's
   scope" sentence is correct for reviewers but will not satisfy the two people who saw the
   crossover slides.
2. **One training seed per arm.** They will ask whether a second seed is feasible before
   2026-10-12. Have the cost ready: ~79 GPU-h and the oracle bill for two arms; probably not, and
   the paper already weights this as the principal limitation.
3. **Matched iterations vs matched cost.** The EDA has a budget sweep (K=5 is behind at small
   budgets and draws level at ~23–27 GPU-h). The iterations-only axis was a deliberate 08-27
   decision; be ready to defend it, or to add one sentence in Limitations that the iso-compute
   reading exists and where it points.
4. **No human MI coder.** The README lists a small human-coded sample as optional; a supervisor
   with clinical contacts may want it. Twenty endpoint conversations, two arms, one coder, is a
   week's work and would close the paper's most-repeated caveat.
5. **Saturation: contribution or caveat?** Ask them directly (A2).
