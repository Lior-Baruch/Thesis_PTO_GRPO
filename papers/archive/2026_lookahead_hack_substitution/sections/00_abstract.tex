\begin{abstract}
Training a small language model to conduct Motivational Interviewing (MI) against a validated
questionnaire scored by a larger LLM produces a specific reward hack: the therapist learns to
flatter. A natural fix is to stop scoring the therapist's turn in isolation and instead score the
$K$-turn trajectory the turn leads to, so that praise which goes nowhere is exposed. We run that
intervention as a controlled arm --- same optimiser, same candidate budget, same oracle, $K \in
\{0, 5\}$ --- over eleven matched training iterations, and evaluate 96 persona-matched
conversations at each under two independent LLM judges, one of which never participated in
training.
%
Look-ahead works on the channel it targets, and keeps working. Over-praise is $2.42$ acts per
session lower under $K{=}5$ at the matched endpoint ($d_z{=}0.89$; $d_z{=}1.00$ under the held-out
judge), the gap is absent for six iterations and then opens and widens, and it is visible upstream
in what the reward itself selects for. Running to the horizon at which the unmitigated arm's
flattery compounds fastest shows this is prevention rather than delay.
%
It does not follow that the therapist commits fewer MI violations. Unsolicited advice and
directing rise by close to what flattery gives up: for eight iterations the total number of
MI-inconsistent acts per session is statistically identical between the arms ($3.45$ vs.\ $3.46$;
$d_z{=}0.004$, $p{=}.97$). At the endpoint the primary oracle does score the totals apart
($d_z{=}0.45$, $p{=}.0003$) --- but the held-out judge, which agrees on the sign and significance
of every individual component, scores the same total null ($d_z{=}0.10$, $p{=}.17$), because it
weights the substituted behaviour more heavily. The reward being optimised is indifferent
throughout.
%
Two lessons follow, and the second is the one we did not expect. Reward-hacking mitigations must be
scored on an aggregate nominated before the intervention, not on the channel that motivated them.
And once an intervention changes the \emph{composition} of a failure rather than its volume,
aggregate comparisons stop being grader-invariant even when every component is: two judges can
agree on all the parts and still disagree on the sum.
\end{abstract}
