\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One training run per cell.} Every effect size is computed across the 96 personas
within a single training run per arm. There are no training-seed replicates, so run-to-run
variance is unmeasured, and ``the ranking flips'' cannot be fully separated from ``these four
runs landed this way.'' The interaction's size ($\dz \approx 0.8$--$1.0$), its agreement across
two graders, and the replicated evaluation draws mitigate but do not remove this; it is the
limitation to weight most heavily.

\paragraph{Evaluation draws.} Therapist decoding is unseeded and each state was evaluated on one
96-conversation sample. The two winning endpoints were re-drawn (36 same-policy contrasts, none
significant, max $|\dz|\,0.216$) and every headline contrast survives substitution; the other 40
states remain single draws, and a re-draw bounds evaluation noise only.

\paragraph{The optimizers differ in more than the loss.} \PTO\ and \GRPO\ necessarily differ in
their own hyperparameters (DPO $\beta$ vs.\ KL $\beta$, pair threshold vs.\ group size
semantics), and no tuning budget was spent optimising either arm beyond its published defaults.
``Optimizer'' here means the packaged method as one would deploy it, not the loss in isolation;
a tuned \PTO-$\Kf$ might behave differently.

\paragraph{$K \in \{0, 5\}$ only.} No dose--response between the two horizons and nothing above
5; nothing here says 5 is optimal or that the crossover is monotone in $K$.

\paragraph{In-sample personas; coupled generator.} The 96 personas exhaust the persona grid and
serve as both training and evaluation population, so generalisation to unseen patients is
unmeasured. All conversations were generated against a \texttt{gpt-4o-mini} patient; the held-out
judge decouples the grading, not the generation.

\paragraph{LLM-administered instruments; no human coder.} The oracle is highly self-repeatable
(ICC $0.86$--$0.99$ on the measured subset), but repeatability is not validity, and no human MI
coder rated any conversation. The behavioural conclusions lean on a deterministic lexical marker
for exactly this reason, and that marker is itself a deliberately brittle keyword pattern used
as a direction check.

\paragraph{Grader saturation.} \S\ref{sec:measurement}: per-conversation cross-grader agreement
collapses at the late $\Kf$ checkpoints because the trained-against grader loses discriminative
range there. Arm-level orderings survive; per-conversation claims about those states, and
absolute levels near the training oracle's ceiling, should not be reused downstream.

\paragraph{Matched iterations are not matched data.} All contrasts are made at matched
iteration counts. An iteration is 96 conversations for every arm and $M{=}G{=}8$ candidates per
decision point, but \GRPO\ extracts an oracle-scored group from every eligible slice of every
conversation while \PTO\ branches only along its single trunk per conversation, so the
optimizers consume different amounts of oracle-scored signal per iteration by construction: over
the ten iterations, roughly $302$k oracle scoring calls for \GRPO\ vs.\ $100$k for \PTO\ at
$\Kz$ ($3.0\times$) and $290$k vs.\ $122$k at $\Kf$ ($2.4\times$). ``\GRPO\ wins at $\Kf$'' is
therefore a matched-iteration statement, not a matched-data one; we do not equalise that axis,
and the head-to-head null in \S\ref{ssec:winners} is one reason not to over-read it.

\paragraph{The regime comparison is observational.} \S\ref{sec:regime} re-scores conversations
from an earlier experiment that differs from the present one in several ways at once (policy
scale, patient difficulty, training loop); it establishes that the lever's sign varies with
regime, not which ingredient is responsible.
