\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the answer depends on.} ``Does look-ahead help'' turned out to be five
questions. On the \emph{optimizer}: at iteration~5 the held-out judge has PTO ahead at \Kz{}
and GRPO ahead at \Kf{} (DiD \dz{} $0.52$; \S\ref{sec:reward}). On the \emph{grader}: the PTO
\Kz{} edge is one Holm-significant iteration under the training oracle and three under the
held-out judge, and GRPO \Kf{}'s matched-budget lead exists only under the held-out judge
(\S\ref{sec:cost}). On the \emph{budget}: both \Kf{} arms trail at every budget short of the last
one or two of their sweeps (PTO \Kf{} until $19.7$\,h; GRPO \Kf{} until $23.2$\,h, where it
already ties under the training oracle and leads under the held-out judge) and draw level, or
part company, only there. On the \emph{instrument}: at PTO's endpoint WAI-SR's total does not
move while its composition does, and MICI falls under \Kf{} per turn on both graders and per
session on one (for GRPO the per-session drop is on the other grader and the per-turn one is
absent; \S\ref{sec:behaviour}). And on the \emph{patient stratum}: the \Kf{} gains that exist
sit on the personas scripted to warm up, while the held-out judge's \Kz{} PTO edge sits on the
cooperative third.

\paragraph{Why: $K$ acts through the data.} Look-ahead hands the optimizer a different signal,
not a different loss, and three properties of that signal account for most of what we saw. It
is \emph{rescaled}: margin and within-group SD rise by the same factor; GRPO's normalization
divides the scale back out, PTO's absolute $\tau$ turns it into more pairs. It carries a
\emph{session-closing pressure}: candidates whose tail ends early --- almost always because the
simulated patient closes --- are $21$--$23\%$ less likely to be the argmax, and in PTO that
pressure grows, consistent with PTO \Kf{}'s longer sessions and the length channel its selection
opens. And it \emph{shows the grader the patient's reaction}: the route it closes is praise on
both optimizers, and the one it leaves open --- in PTO, where every component of the trade keeps
its sign across graders --- is directive advice, the failure MI was formulated against
\citep{miller1991mi}; why the simulated patient's reaction spares advice and not praise we have
not tested.

\paragraph{The regime reading of the reproduction.} The poster's ordering survives a modern
grader on its own transcripts (\S\ref{sec:iclr}), so the non-transfer lies in what changed: a 1B
rather than 7B therapist, in bf16 rather than 4-bit; a stronger, less cooperative patient
simulator; a tree seeded at utterance 12 with a prefix sliced from the current policy's own
sessions, rather than grown from the opening; and hyperparameters matched across optimizers
rather than tuned for PTO; the design cannot separate them.

\paragraph{How to report a look-ahead result.} Per optimizer, since the sign is
optimizer-conditional. At matched compute as well as matched iteration, since the lever costs
${\approx}2\times$ and its sign is a function of budget: quote the sweep, not a row. Under a
held-out judge and on instruments outside the reward, since the training oracle under-reports
the harm channel by $1.1$--$4.6\times$ from iteration~3 on. Counts before rates for MI-consistency; endpoints rather
than best-of-$N$. The next steps are a second seed of every arm, intermediate depths
$K \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, GRPO \Kf{} run to ten iterations, and human MITI coding of a stratified
sample.
