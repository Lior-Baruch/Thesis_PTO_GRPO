Three readings worth naming. \textbf{First, the substitution is corroborated by a second
instrument.} The MICI coder's advice-without-permission rise under \Kf{} (\S\ref{sec:aggregate})
reappears in the MITI behaviour counts as \emph{persuade} --- $0.67$ more acts per session under
the primary oracle ($\dz{}=0.52$) and $2.63$ more under the held-out judge ($\dz{}=0.87$), both
Holm-significant --- so the ``flattery becomes advice'' trade is visible to two independently
prompted coders, not an artifact of one rubric's category boundaries. \textbf{Second, most of the
\Kf{} arm's higher per-session MI-consistent counts are session length.} The \Kf{} policy takes
about four more therapist turns per session, and its per-session question and reflection counts
are higher by roughly that factor while the per-turn versions are null --- the one exception being
affirmations, which are genuinely lower under \Kf{} on both measures. \textbf{Third, the
session-shape rows are large, deterministic, and not quality differences.} At iteration~10 the
\Kf{} policy writes longer turns ($810.9$ vs.\ $686.2$ characters) across more therapist turns
($14.4$ vs.\ $10.2$) in longer conversations ($28.7$ vs.\ $20.4$ utterances). That turn-count gap
is the denominator behind every per-turn rate in this paper and is why \S\ref{sec:aggregate}
reports counts first.
