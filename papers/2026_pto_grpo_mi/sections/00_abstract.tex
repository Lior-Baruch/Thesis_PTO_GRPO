\begin{abstract}
When a large-language-model judge supplies the reward for a dialogue policy, two design choices
are usually made independently: the \emph{optimizer} that consumes the reward, and the
\emph{horizon} of transcript the judge is shown. We show they interact strongly enough that
neither can be chosen alone. In a controlled $2 \times 2$ --- preference-tree DPO (\PTO) versus
Group Relative Policy Optimization (\GRPO), each with a turn-level ($\Kz$) or trajectory-level
($\Kf$-turn look-ahead) reward, matched on oracle, personas, group size, temperatures, and
iteration count --- training a 1B-parameter therapist for motivational interviewing against a
simulated patient, the \emph{ranking of the optimizers flips with the horizon}: at $\Kz$ the
preference-tree method wins the matched-endpoint contrast ($+0.51$ of a rubric point,
$\dz\,0.73$, training oracle; $+0.61$, $\dz\,1.27$ under a held-out judge from another model
family), while at $\Kf$ GRPO wins it ($-0.21$, $\dz\,-0.36$; $-0.21$, $\dz\,-0.31$). The
interaction (difference-in-differences $\dz\,0.79$--$0.97$) holds under both graders, and every
cell of it survives an independent re-draw of the winning endpoints' evaluation sets. The flip
is grader-conditional at the top: the training oracle scores \GRPO-$\Kf$ as the grid's best
final state, but under the held-out judge its endpoint is statistically indistinguishable from
\PTO-$\Kz$'s (own-base gains $+1.038$ vs.\ $+1.036$), a paired null that replicates on a second
evaluation draw. The horizon also decides what is learned rather than only how much: under turn-level
reward \emph{both} optimizers drift into unearned praise --- a judge-free lexical marker finds
over-praise in $67\%$ (\GRPO) and $21\%$ (\PTO) of therapist turns at the endpoint --- and
trajectory-level reward suppresses the drift in both, while in \PTO\ part of the inconsistency
relocates to unsolicited advice. Re-scoring an earlier, easier regime of the same task shows the
look-ahead lever helping \PTO\ there, so the interaction is regime-scoped: the horizon is not a
free-standing ``improvement'' whose sign can be reported independently of the optimizer, the
grader, and the regime.
\end{abstract}
